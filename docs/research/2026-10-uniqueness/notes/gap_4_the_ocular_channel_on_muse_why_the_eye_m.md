## Key Question A — Why is `eye_movement_proxy` saturated (score min = max = 1.0 on every real epoch), and what unit does EEG carry when it reaches the feature code?

### Takeaway
The saturation is not a units bug in the decoder: every link of the chain delivers centered microvolts (1450/16383 µV per 14-bit count, midscale 8192 subtracted), and this is exactly the convention BrainFlow converged on in August 2026. The defect is in the feature and its thresholds: `mean|diff(AF7−AF8)|` on raw 256 Hz samples is a broadband high-pass measure whose "strong" threshold of 0.20 µV per sample was fitted to a 1 Hz synthetic sinusoid; on real Muse data the value is 3.4–11.7 µV per sample (dominated by high-frequency noise and the 64 Hz line, not by eye movements), so the score pins at 1.0. The repository owner reached the same diagnosis in the issue thread on 2026-10-09; the fix has not landed. Correct next step: redesign the feature (low-pass ≤5 Hz before differencing, or event-based REM detection on the bipolar frontal derivation), then refit thresholds on a REM night with a reference stager — not "fix scaling" and not "retrain".

### Inferences
- Classification: the saturation is **(ii) thresholds tuned to a synthetic scale** combined with **(iii) a feature that, as designed, cannot be informative about REMs** — not (i) a unit mismatch. Every link of the chain is in centered µV, the BrainFlow August-2026 fixes landed on exactly the same constants, and a first-difference metric is immune to the DC-offset discrepancy between OpenMuse/amused-py and remind-eeg/BrainFlow. The repo therefore does **not** need a scaling fix; "fix scaling, then re-evaluate" would be the wrong recommendation.
- Physics of the defect: `diff()` at 256 Hz is a high-pass with gain ∝ |sin(πf/fs)|, so a 64 Hz line is weighted ≈64× more than a 1 Hz eye movement of equal amplitude, and broadband EMG/ADC noise dominates. Eye movements (0.3–2 Hz, hundreds of ms) add almost nothing to the 30 s mean. Lowering the thresholds to the real 3–12 µV range would not help: the metric would still track noise level, not REM density. The owner's 5 Hz low-pass turns it into something closer to slow-wave/EOG energy (0.09–0.35 µV awake eyes-closed), but a per-sample mean still conflates REMs with frontal slow waves and blinks; an event-based detector (amplitude/duration criteria on the low-passed AF7−AF8, then REM count/density per epoch) is the standard approach in the literature (see Part B) and is what the ZMax validation did on the equivalent F7/F8-to-Fpz montage.
- Implications for the recommendation: (1) zero the weight now (the detector is effectively one-feature, issue #116), (2) redesign the feature as a low-passed bipolar event detector, (3) refit `min/strong` on a REM night against a reference stager, or normalise per night (percentile/z-score) because frontal-to-Fpz referencing attenuates ocular amplitude and the absolute scale varies across nights, (4) re-grid the gate. "Retrain on BOAS AF7-AF8" is a later option, not the fix for a defective hand-crafted feature. The owner's roadmap in #116 agrees on (1)–(4) but has not shipped.
- For `brainflow_source.py` and `openmuse_lsl_source.py`, which pass third-party values straight into `EEGSample` (local lines 463 and 365), values from BrainFlow before 2026-08-18 were ≈4.55× too large with a DC pedestal, and OpenMuse values still carry the ≈725 µV pedestal; the artifact diagnostics centre on the per-epoch median, so this matters less for the proxy than for any absolute-µV threshold such as `artifact_abs_uv_threshold`.

## Key Question B — What does 2021–Oct 2026 evidence say about automated REM-burst / REM-density detection and automated LRLR detection from frontal/frontopolar EEG without EOG?

### Takeaway
Frontal EEG clearly contains eye-movement information: the Dresler lab counted REMs on ZMax F7/F8-to-Fpz with an event detector and got r = 0.66 (counts) / 0.60 (density) against PSG EOG, with systematic over-counting, in a 135-night preprint; the same group's 60-participant multi-centre study scored 154 LRLR signals on the same two frontal channels; and a 5-person study saw eye signals at a single Fpz electrode. But every lucid-dream eye-signal verification I found (Konkoly 2021, Picard-Deland/Esfahani 2024–2026, Raduga 2021–2024) was done by human raters, mostly offline, with majority voting; no peer-reviewed or preprint algorithm for automatic LRLR detection with a sensitivity/false-positive rate exists in the literature I could reach, the standard REM detectors (YASA `rem_detect`, Nagy 2026) are EOG-only, and the only Muse-specific eye-movement attempt is a 2019 student project with no numbers.

### Cited Findings

**(1) REM-burst / REM-density detection from frontal channels**
- ZMax validation (bioRxiv 2023, preprint, v1 2023-08-21, no published DOI as of the fetch): 135 nights, >900 h, 4 datasets; ZMax has two frontal EEG channels "F7-Fpz and F8-Fpz" at 256 Hz; eye movements were detected with the algorithm of Ngo et al. (2015) in SleepTrip on both systems, "ZMax F7 − F8" against PSG "horizontal EOG left − EOG right"; REM counts r = 0.66, p < 0.001, ZMax 831.57 ± 549.31 vs PSG 467.01 ± 511.6 (over-estimation; Bland–Altman bias −364.57 ± 442.80); REM density r = 0.60, bias −2.16 ± 2.33 per epoch; REM mean duration r = 0.12 (p = 0.22) and speed r = 0.15 (p = 0.10) not significant; authors attribute attenuation to F7/F8 being "referenced to a relatively close channel (Fpz)"; detection parameters are only in a supplement I could not read. DreamentoScorer κ 72.18% vs ZLab 59.61% against human staging; SO counts r = 0.74, spindles r = 0.67 — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.08.18.553744](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.08.18.553744v1), [full-text XML](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/early/2023/08/21/2023.08.18.553744.source.xml), [bioRxiv API record](https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.1101/2023.08.18.553744). Offline analysis; peer-review status: preprint.
- Dreamento (MIT licence; SoftwareX 2023, peer-reviewed, DOI 10.1016/j.softx.2023.101595; arXiv 2207.03977 v3 2023-01-02): README lists offline "eye movements during REM + slow-oscillation and spindle detection" using "validated YASA algorithms" and manual marking of "predefined eye signals, e.g., LRLR"; real-time features are spectrogram/periodogram, autoscoring, stimulation and annotations — [dreamento/dreamento](https://github.com/dreamento/dreamento), [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03977). In `OfflineDreamento.py` the only REM-event call is `yasa.rem_detect(loc=…, roc=…, sf=…, hypno=…, include=4, …)` inside the BrainProducts (PSG) branch, fed with EOG channel indices `×1e6`; the Hypnodyne/ZMax branches do not call it; LRLR is a manual event code 5757 in `ImportManualScoring` — [OfflineDreamento.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dreamento/dreamento/main/OfflineDreamento.py) (first 100 k characters read).

### Inferences
- Feasibility of a REM-burst/REM-density feature on Muse AF7/AF8: supported in principle. The ZMax montage (F7/F8 to Fpz) is the closest published analogue to Muse's AF7/AF8 and yielded r ≈ 0.66 for REM counts with an off-the-shelf event detector — good enough for a per-epoch REM-density feature in a multi-feature detector, not good enough as a standalone REM stager (counts over-estimated ≈1.8×, density r = 0.60, durations uncorrelated). Expect the same attenuation and over-counting on Muse, which argues for per-night normalisation or data-fitted thresholds rather than literature constants (YASA's 50–325 µV EOG amplitudes will not transfer to an attenuated frontal derivation).
- Feasibility of automated at-home LRLR verification on Muse: **unproven**. The signal is visible on frontal EEG (154 LRLRs scored on ZMax; 7/8 at a single Fpz electrode), but in every study the detection was human, mostly offline, with 2-of-3 or 3-of-4 majority rules and no published inter-rater statistic for the signals themselves; Konkoly's control data (11 ambiguous + 1 "correct" out of 379 non-lucid trials under expert reading) show that even human raters produce false positives, and no paper reports sensitivity/FPR for an algorithm. There is no public LRLR detector code, no Muse dataset with labelled LRLRs, and the only Muse eye-movement project is awake, threshold-based, and unquantified.
- Recommendation for the report: do not present "signal-verified lucidity at home on a 4-channel headband" as remind-eeg's uniqueness play; flag it as an unproven research direction that would require (a) a REM-night dataset with time-stamped LRLR attempts, (b) an offline detector validated against ≥2 human raters, and (c) a reported FPR on non-lucid REM. A defensible, nearer-term differentiator is the one the repo's own issue thread points to: a validated low-passed bipolar AF7−AF8 REM-density feature (the ZMax approach) feeding the existing stable gate, plus Dreamento-style manual LRLR marking in the morning report — i.e. "LRLR-ready logging", not "LRLR detection".
