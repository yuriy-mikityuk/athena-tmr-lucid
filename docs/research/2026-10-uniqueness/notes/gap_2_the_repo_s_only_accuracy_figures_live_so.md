## Part B — GSSC findings

### Takeaway

GSSC is a peer-reviewed, AGPL-3.0, PyTorch sleep stager (1-D ResNets + bidirectional GRU) trained on 3,314 NSRR polysomnography nights with permutation training that makes it usable from a single EEG channel without EOG; the published model was "mostly trained on the EEG channels C3, C4, F3, F4" — frontal *and* central, mastoid/Fpz-referenced — and reports accuracy "well over 80%" with κ values only in figures/supplement. The repo pins the 2023 PyPI release 0.0.9, whose `mne_infer` returns argmax labels only, and feeds it AF7 as EEG plus AF8 as pseudo-EOG. Independent peer-reviewed benchmarks on clinical PSG rank GSSC above YASA (REM F1 0.76 vs 0.35), and one 2026 preprint finds it the most robust model on a single-frontal-channel portable device (with EOG/EMG, fine-tuned κ 0.58), but no study evaluates GSSC on forehead-only or consumer headband EEG.

### Cited Findings

**B1. How the repository uses GSSC (local files, read-only)**

- `scripts/_gssc_stage.py` imports `from gssc.infer import EEGInfer`, builds an MNE `RawArray` from the `.npz` (all channels typed `eeg`, µV→V), and calls `ei = EEGInfer(use_cuda=False); stages, _times = ei.mne_infer(raw, eeg=[args.eeg], eog=eog)`; it maps stage indices `{0: Wake, 1: N1, 2: N2, 3: N3, 4: REM}` and writes `proba_rem = 1.0 if REM else 0.0`, `confidence = 1.0`. Docstring: "GSSC's mne_infer returns argmax stages (no per-class probabilities), so proba_rem here is a hard 0/1 label". It monkey-patches `torch.load` to `weights_only=False` because "GSSC 0.0.9 pickles its bundled net classes". Defaults: `--eeg AF7`, `--eog none` — [scripts/_gssc_stage.py](https://github.com/yuriy-mikityuk/remind-eeg/blob/main/scripts/_gssc_stage.py)
- `scripts/validate_rem_detection.py` reconstructs AF7/AF8/TP9/TP10 from `raw_amused.bin`, zero-fills BLE gaps >0.5 s, polyphase-resamples to 100 Hz (declared exactly 100 Hz), saves all four channels to the `.npz`, but passes only `--eeg` (default `AF7`) and `--eog` (default **`AF8`**) to the stager subprocess for both references; TP9/TP10 are never used by either stager. It flags a "degenerate reference" if REM <5 %, N3 <2 % or <3 stages over ≥240 epochs; it computes binary REM-vs-not precision/recall/F1/κ for `p_rem ≥ enter`, `≥ exit`, `≥ 0.5`, best-F1, a 0.05-step sweep, ROC-AUC, and "SYSTEM VIEW" metrics of the simulated `StableRemGate` (`gate_open`) against reference REM. Its GSSC caveat: "GSSC is frontal-trained (a much better montage fit than YASA), but still an automated proxy, not PSG." — [scripts/validate_rem_detection.py](https://github.com/yuriy-mikityuk/remind-eeg/blob/main/scripts/validate_rem_detection.py)
- `scripts/_yasa_stage.py` (fallback/default reference) uses `yasa.SleepStaging(raw, eeg_name=AF7, eog_name=AF8)` and does expose per-epoch REM probability and max-class confidence; docstring: YASA "was trained on central electrodes (C3/C4) referenced to the mastoids or Fpz; the Muse gives only frontal (AF7/AF8) and temporal (TP9/TP10) channels referenced near Fpz, and there is no EMG." — [scripts/_yasa_stage.py](https://github.com/yuriy-mikityuk/remind-eeg/blob/main/scripts/_yasa_stage.py)
- Install path documented as `python -m venv .venv-gssc && .venv-gssc/bin/pip install gssc` (i.e., the PyPI release), with `.venv-gssc/` gitignored ("Isolated venv for the GSSC frontal reference stager") — [docs/USAGE.md](https://github.com/yuriy-mikityuk/remind-eeg/blob/main/docs/USAGE.md); [.gitignore](https://github.com/yuriy-mikityuk/remind-eeg/blob/main/.gitignore)

**B3. GSSC primary publication (peer-reviewed)**

- Hanna J, Flöel A. "An accessible and versatile deep learning-based sleep stage classifier." *Frontiers in Neuroinformatics* 17:1086634 (2023); received 2022-11-01, accepted 2023-02-13, published 2023-03-02; DOI 10.3389/fninf.2023.1086634; PMID 36938361; PMCID PMC10017438; Methods article; 13 citations in Europe PMC. Affiliations: Greifswald University Hospital; DZNE Rostock/Greifswald. Preprint: bioRxiv 2022.08.24.504155 (2022) — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2023.1086634/full); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36938361); [bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.08.24.504155)
- Architecture: separate one-dimensional ResNets for EEG and EOG (EEG ResNet four blocks, EOG shallower); 30-s epochs downsampled to 85.33 Hz = 2,560 samples; 1,280-length representation per signal → mixing/compression to 512; two jointly trained heads: context-free (three fully connected layers) and context-aware (bidirectional GRU with hidden state); chin EMG tested in prototypes, not used — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2023.1086634/full)
- Training data: public sleepdata.org (NSRR) PSG only — SHHS2 936/3,295 nights (C3-A2, C4-A1), CFS 730/730 (C3-Fpz, C4-Fpz), NCHSDB 665/3,984 (F3-M2, F4-M1, C3-M2, C4-M1), WSC 983/8,794 (F3-M2, C3-M2, O1-M2), all with left/right EOG; total 3,314 nights, 29,299 h, 3,515,889 epochs; split 80/10/10 (2,652/331/331). Permutation training: every PSG used multiple times as single EEG channel, HEOG alone, or EEG+HEOG, with polarity flips "to simulate changes in EEG reference"; inference uses "permutation consensus" (keep the most confident permutation by cross-entropy of the log-softmax output) — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2023.1086634/full)
- Results (text): "Mean accuracy is well over 80% in all cases, and outperforms YASA by at least a few points across all datasets." External test on DREEM (DOD-H 25 healthy, DOD-O 55 OSA; five-scorer consensus): GSSC 0.7 % below U-Sleep on Healthy, 2 % above on Obstructive (n.s.); "significantly better than YASA and Stephansen et al. (2018)"; light sleep (N1+N2) 91.5 % / 91 %. Per-channel: "All EEG channels perform above 80% mean accuracy"; GSSC channels "cluster within a few percent above or below the baseline [C3]"; "For YASA on the other hand, only channel C4 performed comparably to their recommended C3 channel, and the others tended to be about 5% less accurate." N3 over-prediction +2.9 % (p = 0.003). Forward-only (real-time-capable) inference starts ~2 % below bidirectional and narrows to ~1 %; context-free ~4 % → ~8 % below. **Cohen's κ values appear only in Figures 3/6 and Supplementary Table 1; no numeric κ or REM-specific F1/sensitivity is in the article text.** Speed: "YASA processes a full night in seconds … GSSC is slower, even with a GPU. On a CPU, a full night can take minutes"; single-channel mode costs ~1–2 % accuracy. Limitations named: slower than YASA; non-converging validation (final weights = mean of three best epochs); smaller training set than U-Sleep (19,924 PSGs); N1 weak; N3 bias. Wearables/consumer devices are not discussed; "portable, 1–2 channel miniature systems designed for home testing" is mentioned once in the Discussion — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2023.1086634/full); [PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10017438/fullTextXML)
- Original-study macro-F1 as tabulated by an independent group: GSSC 0.82 ± 0.08 (DOD-H) and 0.80 ± 0.11 (DOD-O); YASA 0.79 ± 0.11 and 0.74 ± 0.12 — [Hanif et al. 2025, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70048)
- DREEM Open Datasets: Guillot A, Sauvet F, During EH, Thorey V. *IEEE TNSRE* 28(9):1955–1965 (2020), DOI 10.1109/TNSRE.2020.3011181; "DOD-H including 25 healthy volunteers and DOD-O including 55 patients suffering from obstructive sleep apnea"; "scored by 5 sleep technologists from different sleep centers"; human F1 86.8 % (DOD-H) / 84.8 % (DOD-O) — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Dreem%20Open%20Datasets%22&format=json&resultType=core)

### Inferences (Part B)

- **What κ 0.30 is.** It is Cohen's κ of a *binary* label (gate open vs. closed, per 30-s epoch) against GSSC's REM/non-REM label on one night, computed by the harness's own `_cohen_kappa(tp, fp, fn, tn)`. It is not a five-stage κ and is not comparable to the five-class κ 0.45 (YASA on ZMax), κ 0.58 (fine-tuned GSSC on SOMNOtouch) or κ 0.76 (U-Sleep on PSG) quoted elsewhere in the report. With REM at 13.6 % prevalence, a precision of 0.30 at recall 0.80 mechanically yields κ ≈ 0.30; the figure says "two of three gate-open epochs are not REM according to GSSC", nothing more.
- **What it can mean.** It is a valid internal sanity check: the detector's p_rem ranks GSSC-REM above non-REM (AUC 0.758, #112), GSSC's hypnogram is physiologically plausible (13.6 % REM, 7.9 % N3) where YASA's was not, and the old thresholds demonstrably over-fired. It legitimately motivated raising the gate thresholds.
- **What it cannot mean.** It cannot be read as agreement with PSG or expert scoring, for four compounding reasons: (1) the reference is itself unvalidated on this montage — GSSC was trained on mastoid/Fpz-referenced C3/C4/F3/F4 from clinical PSG, while the harness gives it AF7 (more anterior than F3/F4, referenced near Fpz with a short inter-electrode distance) and a second EEG channel, AF8, presented as "EOG", which the README neither trained for nor recommends (only HEOG, or L/R EOG alone, are documented); (2) the only frontal-channel evidence for GSSC is Gunter 2026, where even with real EOG, chin EMG and fine-tuning the REM F1 is ~0.71 and out-of-box κ across models is 0.21–0.54 — so GSSC's REM labels on AF7 alone are plausibly noisy, and reference noise attenuates any measured agreement by an unknown amount; (3) the 0.90/0.85 thresholds were fitted on the same night the κ is reported for (in-sample); (4) n = 1 subject, 1 night. The repo's own documents say the first two of these (the harness prints "still an automated proxy, not PSG"), but the phrase "frontal-trained" in `presets.py`, `validate_rem_detection.py` and `docs/USAGE.md` overstates the montage match: GSSC's README says *mostly C3, C4, F3, F4*, i.e., frontal **and** central, and F3/F4 come from only two of the four training datasets (NCHSDB, WSC). "Trained with frontal derivations among others" is the exact statement.
- **The pinned version matters.** The repo installs PyPI 0.0.9 (2023-09-28), which returns argmax labels only; the harness therefore writes `proba_rem` as 0/1 and cannot weight epochs by GSSC confidence or run a soft comparison, whereas the YASA path keeps probabilities. The maintainer added probability output on `main` in 2025 but never released 0.1.0; the repo's two-tuple unpack `stages, _times = ei.mne_infer(...)` would raise on a source install of `main`, so the published behaviour is coupled to a 3-year-old, torch-2.6-incompatible wheel that the script patches with `weights_only=False`.
- **Is GSSC-over-YASA defensible?** Yes, on three independent grounds: (a) design — GSSC supports EEG-only single-channel inference by construction (permutation training) and includes F3/F4 in training, while YASA's model is built around one central EEG channel and, per the GSSC paper, loses ~5 % accuracy on non-central channels; (b) the repo's own empirical check — on the same Muse night YASA produced a degenerate hypnogram (1.9 % REM, 0.5 % N3, flagged by the harness's guard) and GSSC a plausible one; (c) independent benchmarks — GSSC beats YASA on REM in insomnia PSG (F1 0.76 vs 0.35), on athletes' PSG (macro-F1 0.76 vs 0.70) and is the most robust model on a single-frontal portable montage (Gunter 2026). What is **not** defensible is any inference from κ 0.30 to the detector's true REM accuracy, because no GSSC-vs-PSG number exists for forehead-only consumer EEG, and the YASA κ 0.45 on ZMax (five-class, home, generic stager) shows that generic stagers degrade substantially on forehead montages — a degradation that would apply to GSSC's labels on AF7 too, in an unknown amount. The comparison therefore "bounds nothing" in the critic's phrase; it is a consistency check between two unvalidated systems.
- The report's statement that GSSC is "the automated stager behind every one of those numbers" should be qualified: the ROC-AUC 0.46, 1.9 % REM and 0 / 959 figures in #112/#113 are against YASA; everything from the "GSSC replaces YASA" comment onwards (AUC 0.758, κ 0.26/0.30, precision 0.15/0.27/0.30, 342/959, the #116 AUC table) is against GSSC.

### Gaps (Part B)

- Numeric κ and per-stage (REM) metrics from the GSSC paper are only in Figures 3/4/6, Supplementary Figure 4 and Supplementary Table 1, which the text fetches did not expose; the only tabulated original-study numbers found are the macro-F1 values reproduced by Hanif 2025 (0.82/0.80). Whether GSSC's REM κ on PSG is ~0.8 (typical of U-Sleep-class models) is therefore unverified here.
- The channel derivations GSSC received from the DOD datasets (F3-F4, Fp1-F3, etc. vs. C3-M2) are not stated in the extracted text; the DOD montage itself (full PSG with 12 EEG derivations, per my background knowledge) could not be confirmed from the abstract.
- GSSC's out-of-the-box κ on the single-frontal SOMNOtouch montage (Gunter 2026, Table 6) is not in the extracted text; only the cross-model range 0.21–0.54 and the fine-tuned κ 0.58 are.
- No source evaluates GSSC on Muse, ZMax, Dreem-headband or any forehead-only EEG without EOG/EMG; Parry & Briganti 2026 (ZMax) tested YASA and Dreamento only. The effect of presenting AF8 as "EOG" to GSSC is undocumented anywhere.
- The actual GSSC invocation for #115 (whether `--eog none` was passed) is not logged; the inference that AF8 acted as pseudo-EOG rests on the code defaults and the documented command.
- `sleep_staging_tools.md` and `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` contain no mention of GSSC or Greifswald (grep on 2026-10-21), confirming the critic's observation; the facts above are the material to fill that hole.
