## Method

Each initiative was scored 1–5 on four criteria:

- **E** — evidence strength in the notes (verified facts only; where a `verify_*.md` file corrects a note, the correction counts).
- **U** — real uniqueness versus the competitors the notes name (EazzZyLearn_pc, Dreamento, COsleep, home-tmr, SMACC, Portiloop, Taran-Douley, southsko/muse-sleep-dashboard, ezscore-f, muse-lsl / OpenMuse / BrainFlow, InteraXon Dream Recall and research TMR, Northwestern home TLR), always scoped as "of the repositories inspected".
- **F** — feasibility for one maintainer with a Muse S Athena, a Polar H10, one macOS machine, open data, and no sleep lab.
- **H** — scientific honesty: the deliverable is a measurement, a provenance record, a fidelity or safety metric, never an efficacy claim.

F and H are gating: anything at 2 or below on either is rejected or deferred. E and U rank the rest; ties are broken by dependency order and by size (smaller first). Duplicates across lenses were merged into one entry that names every source lens. Proposals that duplicate code already in the checkout were removed, after a live `ls`/`grep` check listed in the next section.

### 1. Replace the saturated eye-movement proxy with a low-passed bipolar AF7−AF8 REM-event feature, refit, and publish the first-cue latency budget

From ST #1 (primary), with SC #1 step 2, the fixture specification in CO #3 / G4, and the remainder of ST #2 step 4; closes #116, answers #167. The notes' single best-established defect: `eye_movement_proxy` carries 0.25 of the detector weight and scores 1.0 on every real epoch because the unfiltered first difference on 256-Hz samples is a broadband high-pass measure compared against thresholds (0.05–0.20 µV per sample) fitted to a synthetic 1-Hz sinusoid; the #116 per-feature AUC is 0.500 and the code was confirmed verbatim (VRI A8; `eeg_features.py` 305–320). The fix is the owner's own agreed plan: zero the weight in its own PR so the gate change is traceable; add a 0.3–5 Hz band-passed bipolar event detector with a per-night robust (MAD-based) amplitude threshold, removing the ≈63.9-Hz optics line first on optics presets, emitting `rem_event_count`, `rem_density_per_min`, `last_rem_event_age_s`, `rem_burst_active` and event timestamps; add the fixture that would have caught the bug (biphasic opposite-polarity 20–60 µV deflections on 10–20 µV RMS 1/f noise with a 40 µV 64-Hz line; assert the old proxy saturates and the new feature does not); refit via `scripts/validate_rem_detection.py` on at least two REM-bearing `p21` nights the maintainer records at home against the existing GSSC venv; re-grid presets; update `docs/data_model.md`. Add a replay table of first-cue latency per `--stride-seconds` value, since the flag and the wall-time gate already exist. Unique among the inspected projects: none exposes a REM-density feature on a consumer headband; the nearest analogue, Esfahani 2023 on ZMax F7−F8 (counts r = 0.66, density r = 0.60 versus PSG EOG, ≈1.8× over-count), is offline. Feasible: about 200 lines plus tests, two overnight recordings. Honest only under two rules: report gains as AUC against an automated proxy, never as REM accuracy, and keep the feature a supporting term in a conjunction — blinks (wake/N1) and frontal slow waves (N3) share the band, and AF7/AF8 referenced near Fpz attenuate horizontal EOG.

### 2. PSG-anchored validation of the detector and gate on open headband data (BOAS), with a reusable external-label harness

From SC #1 (primary) and ST #2, plus the harness and split-definition parts of CO #8 (the public results table is deferred, see below). The repository's only accuracy figures come from one EEG-only night against GSSC with thresholds fitted on that same night, and they live only in PR #115 (VRI A6/C1); GSSC was trained on C3/C4/F3/F4 and has no published evaluation on forehead-only EEG, so κ 0.30 is a consistency check between two unvalidated systems. BOAS (OpenNeuro ds005555; CC0; BIDS; 128 nights from 100 participants; Bitbrain Ikon AF7/AF8 at 256 Hz plus IMU and a pulse channel; consensus of three AASM scorers with a fourth tie-breaker) is the one open dataset that lets a lab-less maintainer put a PSG-consensus number on the method. Deliverables: a BIDS-headband loader into `MuseFrame` (TP9/TP10 absent — already a normal case for the pipeline); `validate_rem_detection.py --labels` for external hypnograms with stager identity, versions and `--eeg/--eog` flags logged into the metrics JSON; subject-level splits committed as `splits.json`, never intra-subject; per-night and pooled REM κ/F1/precision at recall 0.80, gate-open precision, REM-onset-to-gate-open latency, false-open minutes, per-feature AUC; a `boas_v1` preset beside `p21`, whose comment keeps "tuned on one night vs GSSC"; derived 30-s label CSVs under `data/validation_public/boas/` (CC0 permits, no raw EEG); a strictly causal feature-level classifier in the existing versioned `ml_rem_detector` artifact format, with a fine-tune path from the user's own `annotate-template` rows or imported hypnograms (initiative 6). Also run GSSC, YASA and ezscore-f themselves against the BOAS consensus — the first public number for "GSSC on forehead-only EEG" (SC #2 item 7). Unique: no open REM-gated system reports its own detector against PSG consensus, and no benchmark exists for causal frontal REM detection (SS KQ3 gap); ezscore-f is a ZMax-trained offline stager an independent group could not install. Feasibility 3: roughly 36 GB and months of part-time work, but entirely software and open data. Honesty requires the framing the notes insist on: Ikon referenced to the mastoids is not Muse referenced near Fpz, so the result is "transfer-validated on a frontal montage", never "Muse-validated"; a Muse+PSG night remains something to seek, not to plan; and REMind's present detector is published as the (expected to be poor) baseline, which is the point.

### 3. Cue-locked arousal scoring with matched silent controls as the primary safety outcome

From SC #4 (primary) and the scoring half of ST #3; the two-way volume controller is deferred (see rejected). This carries the strongest literature in the whole note set: an arousal within about 10 s of a cue turns a memory benefit into forgetting (Whitmore & Paller 2023 — arousals after 43.0 % of soft ≈28 dBA cues and 57.9 % of loud ones, with corrected figures in VTS row 9); home TLR cues raised movement events per cue to 0.19 versus 0.10 and "often woke participants" (Konkoly 2024); multisensory REM cues disrupted 23.7 % of cued REM periods (Picard-Deland/Dresler 2024 preprint); Coon 2026 asks for logged stimuli and arousals with stricter settings in REM. The repository's `ArousalGuard` is prospective and epoch-level, and `docs/validation.md` lists guard-burden counts and a "cue-window motion count" but no per-cue EEG arousal metric and no control-window comparison (lines 41–53 checked). Build `reports/cue_arousal.py` as a retrospective, replay-based scorer: for every scheduler `play`, a 10-s post-cue window against a ≈20-s pre-cue baseline for frontal alpha (8–12 Hz) and beta (15–30 Hz) relative-power rise, a high-frequency EMG-band proxy with the 64-Hz optics line excluded on `p1034`, an IMU burst, and an HR jump when PPG is present; a matched silent window per cue in the same gate state with no cue within ±60 s; joins with `awakening_events.jsonl`; outcomes `arousal_proxy_per_cue` by flag type, cue-attributed awakening rate, fraction of REM periods with any disruption, REM-period termination within 60 s of a cue, and dose tables by effective volume, inter-cue interval and cue index, written into `pilot4_summary.json` and a new safety table in `docs/validation.md`; a Pilot 3 variant that injects synthetic cue events into a replayed night and asserts window placement; a one-page `docs/safety_outcomes.md` putting the literature's benchmark values beside the repo's definitions. Unique: no open engine computes arousal-per-cue from its own logs with silent controls (home-tmr: watch motion only, stale; COsleep: disengages but publishes no statistics; SMACC: experimenter-driven; EazzZyLearn: no arousal input). Feasibility 4: the band-power, stillness and HR features exist per epoch; the work is sample-aligned buffering around cue timestamps in the Pilot 4 loop, control sampling, joins and tables, all replay-testable. Honesty 4: forehead-only EEG lacks occipital alpha and chin EMG, so every flag is a proxy with no validation against PSG-scored arousals; volume is a software gain, not dB SPL; and nothing here is a benefit metric — it is what makes the first real audio night interpretable at all.

### 4. Sham and no-cue night arms, N-of-1 block randomisation, delayed retest and blind incorporation coding

From SC #5 and SC #8 (primary) and ST #5, including its LRLR-ready manual marking. The item-level cued/uncued randomiser with structurally unschedulable controls is the repository's most rigorous feature, but the field's control design is night-level: Konkoly 2024 Exp 2 interleaved untrained-sound and no-cue nights with participants blind to the control nights; Konkoly 2026 names "no no-cue night" and incomplete blinding as limitations and found that blind-rated conceptual incorporation, not cueing, predicted solving; REM-cued benefits appeared only at one week (Pereira 2023) or ten days (Rakowska 2021); Göldi & Rasch 2019 saw a benefit only on night 3; SCRIBE 2016 / CENT 2015 and Konigorski 2026 give single-case reporting frameworks and no N-of-1 TMR/TLR study exists. None of the inspected projects implements sealed night arms on top of physiology-gated cueing (COsleep: sham/stim blinding only, stale; Taran-Douley lists sham nights as unimplemented; brainwave_opensync randomises arms without physiology). Build: `protocol/night_schedule.py` — a seeded sequence of {trained-cue, untrained-sound sham, no-cue} blocks with washout nights and a hash commitment revealed only by an explicit unblinding command; an `arm` field in every scheduler and audio JSONL event; sham selects the untrained cue from the cue library under identical gate, guard, timing and volume rules; an `ensure_schedulable()`-style guarantee that a `no_cue` night cannot emit `play`; Pilot 3/4 validator checks that the executed arm matches the sealed schedule; arm-blind morning pages (the Morning tab already hides `cue_condition`) with "did a sound wake you" and cue-perception items; `retest_wave` (`morning|day7|day10`) in `morning_retest.py` and `missing_delayed_retest` in `limitations`; a rater coding schema (sound-cue, direct conceptual, indirect conceptual incorporation) exported without cue IDs or conditions, with rater ID and agreement when two codings exist; `reports/nof1_analysis.py` with per-arm descriptives, night-to-night autocorrelation and first-night flags, a permutation or simple Bayesian interval, and `limitations` extended with `no_sham_nights`, `unbalanced_arms`, `unblinded_early`, `single_subject`, `sham_detectable`, `carry_over_possible`; a SCRIBE/CENT checklist rendered into the Markdown report and an OSF preregistration template built from the protocol manifest of initiative 7 plus the sealed schedule hash. Optional and explicitly non-automatic: a morning "attempted eye signal at ≈HH:MM" field with an offline ±60 s plot of the low-passed AF7−AF8 trace from initiative 1 for manual marking, exported as an EDF+ annotation via initiative 6 — the notes find no LRLR detector with a reported sensitivity or false-positive rate in 2021–2026 (G4 B). Feasibility 4: pure protocol and report code in the existing dataclass plus versioned-JSON pattern, synthetic tests, no new signal processing. Honesty 4: at n = 1 the experimenter cannot be blind, a sham still delivers sound, the untrained sound may be recognised, carry-over and habituation are real, and every output stays a single-case description; preregistration documents intent, it does not create generalisability. Its value is realised only when audio nights run, so it ranks below 1–3, but it should exist before the first Pilot 5 series.

### 9. A versioned, labelled synthetic night corpus in Athena TAG format (`muse_tmr.testing`) with a hardware-free `demo`

From CO #3 and step 3 of CO #2, using the fixture specification of ST #1 / G4. `write_synthetic_recording()` is duplicated in three test files and writes zero-filled packets — format-valid, physiologically empty — which is exactly why the saturated eye proxy passed every test (VRI A16; G4 A); no public Muse overnight dataset exists on Zenodo, PhysioNet, OpenNeuro or the web (VWR row 18). Build `muse_tmr.testing.synthetic`: a seeded generator driven by a scripted hypnogram (8 h, 4–5 REM periods, N1/N2/N3, wake bouts) emitting TAG_EEG_4CH / ACCGYRO / OPTICS packets with stage-typical content — 1/f background at 10–20 µV RMS, N3 delta, REM theta with low alpha plus bipolar opposite-polarity 20–60 µV AF7/AF8 deflections of 0.3–1.5 s, wake alpha and blinks, IMU bursts at arousals and position changes, PPG pulse at a scripted heart rate, the ≈63.9-Hz optics line at 20–40 µV whenever an optics preset is active, BLE gaps, #160-style repeated and lost subpackets, battery decay — writing `raw_amused.bin`, `metadata.json`, `events.jsonl` plus ground truth `hypnogram.csv`, `arousals.csv`, `generation.json`; a scenario library generated in CI (`clean_night`, `fragmented_rem`, `no_rem_nap`, `battery_death_2h29`, `optics_line`, `bad_contact_af7`, `movement_heavy`, `short_allow_short`), each ≤10 MB; replacement of the duplicated helpers; a CI end-to-end job (Pilot 1 validator passes, Pilot 3 shows zero uncued `play`, the HTML report renders, detector-vs-generated-hypnogram tracked as a regression number); `muse-tmr demo` running replay → features → detector → gate → Pilot 3 with mock audio → report as the README's first command; `docs/synthetic_data.md`. It supplies the fixtures initiatives 1, 3 and 4 need, the export fixture for 6, and a decoder test oracle for amused-py, OpenMuse, BrainFlow and muse-rs. None of the inspected projects ships a labelled synthetic overnight corpus in device format (southsko's smoke recordings are unlabelled; Taran-Douley simulates on PSG; EazzZyLearn re-streams a user's CSV). Feasibility 5: no hardware, about two weeks. Honesty 3 is why it ranks here and not higher: synthetic agreement will be misread as validation unless every artefact prints "synthetic input, not sleep", and a detector tuned on the simulator can overfit the author's assumptions about Fpz-referenced REM morphology, which real headbands attenuate and over-count (VWR row 6); it is test infrastructure, never evidence, and must never include fragments of real nights.

### 10. ECG-referenced agreement analysis of Athena forehead optics HR against the Polar H10, with quality flags that the guard respects

From ST #7 steps 1–2 only; duty-cycling and exploratory ΔHbO are rejected below. The repository already records Athena optics and Polar H10 ECG/RR with clock anchors in the same calibration runs, and `ppg_features` derives peak-based HR, an RMSSD proxy and sudden-HR-change that the guard's HR-jump rule consumes, yet nothing quantifies agreement, and no open project has validated Athena forehead PPG against ECG (muse-monitor's HR/HRV is "exploratory", OpenMuse's roadmap still reads "validate the best PPG signal extraction method", BrainFlow deliberately does not convert optics). Build `reports/optics_agreement.py` and `muse-tmr analyze-optics-agreement`: per-epoch Muse optics HR/IBI versus Polar RR (bias, limits of agreement, MAE, coverage by `ppg_signal_quality`, per optics channel) on calibration runs and optics sessions the maintainer can make at a desk, committing metric tables only; a `ppg_confidence` field so the guard ignores low-quality epochs; channel documentation in `docs/data_model.md`. Feasibility 4: both devices are owned and the data path exists. Evidence 3 and last place because the payoff for the REM core is modest: nights run EEG+IMU only on `p21` for battery reasons (p1034 ≈23 %/h), so PPG is absent when it would matter, and the literature positions PPG as a corroboration and arousal signal, not a REM fix (WR KQ4). Honest if confined to HR/IBI agreement — no SpO₂, no HbO/HbR, no sleep-stage claims.
