# Verification of `wearable_rem_physiology_open_science.md` (adversarial fact-check, 2026-10-21)

This file confirms or corrects the factual claims in
`research_notes/Уникальность remind eeg среди аналогов/wearable_rem_physiology_open_science.md`.
It does not replace that file; it sits next to it. **The report writer must prefer the
statements in the "Verdict detail / corrected statement" column below over the wording in
`wearable_rem_physiology_open_science.md` wherever the two differ**, and must not repeat
the misattributed ZMax sentence (row 17), the wrong YASA URL (row 16), the "license not
stated" remark about ezscore-f (row 8) or the Philips age-group claim (row S10) in their
original form.

Method: each claim was checked against the URL the notes cite for it (PMC/bioRxiv/medRxiv
full text, GitHub README, Zenodo record, vendor page), fetched on 2026-10-21. PDFs
(Konkoly 2021, Philips whitepaper) were converted with `pdftotext` and grepped for the
quoted sentences. GitHub facts come from the GitHub search API; publication status of
preprints from the bioRxiv/medRxiv API (`published` field); DOIs from Crossref; the BOAS
licence from OpenNeuro's GraphQL API. The categorical "no public Muse sleep dataset" claim
was tested against the Zenodo API, the PhysioNet published-projects API, the
OpenNeuroDatasets GitHub organisation and one web search. No claim was accepted from
memory. Line numbers (`l.`) refer to `wearable_rem_physiology_open_science.md`.

Summary: 30 claims checked (18 primary, 12 secondary spot checks): 21 CONFIRMED,
7 CORRECTED, 2 UNVERIFIED. Five corrections change a conclusion a reader would draw:
(1) Mota-Rolim 2019 does not say ZMax's REM algorithm lacks public technical detail; that
sentence is about REM-Dreamer, and the paper calls ZMax's documentation "available and
comprehensive" (row 17); (2) ezscore-f's code is MIT-licensed, not unlicensed (row 8);
(3) BOAS is CC0 on OpenNeuro and its scoring paper is 2026 (row 12); (4) the Philips
"effect in younger, absent in older" claim is not in the cited whitepaper, whose pilot only
enrolled 30–50-year-olds (row S10); (5) IEEE document 10123829 is confirmed to be the
InteraXon NER 2023 paper, so the vendor's κ 0.78 claim rests on a peer-reviewed conference
paper, not only a blog (row 3). The other two corrections are a wrong GitHub URL for YASA
(row 16) and a 16-bit vs 14-bit EEG slip in an inference (row S4).

## Claims that are technically true but misleading in context

- **A. Paraphrase presented as a quote (l.88, l.170).** "automation for home use is still needed" is not in Esfahani et al. 2024; the preprint says "Conducting such study outside the laboratory would require further enhancement of the automation process" and specifies artifact rejection, automatic arousal detection and near-real-time REM autoscoring. The report should paraphrase without quotation marks or use the real sentence.
- **B. "REM counts correlate r ≈ 0.6–0.66 with EOG" (l.74, l.77).** The correlations in Esfahani 2023 are between automatic REM detections on ZMax F7–F8 and on PSG horizontal EOG, pooled across datasets; REM duration and speed did *not* correlate. Fine as stated, but it is agreement between two automatic detectors, not a validation of REM-burst detection against manual EOG scoring.
- **C. Parry & Briganti figures (l.48).** The κ 0.371/0.450, 60.0 % accuracy and REM recall 0.44/0.62 are OK-subgroup (N = 74) figures, not whole-cohort; U-Sleep κ 0.761 is for N = 71; the abstract's failure rate is 10 % (FAIL group) while 7 % is the near-complete-dropout subset. A v2 (31 Jul 2026) exists and was not read.
- **D. Lanthier "2025" (l.11).** Online 12 Dec 2025, but the print issue is vol. 7(1), Jan 2026; some repositories cite 2026. Either year is defensible if the DOI is given.
- **E. Arnal 2020 (l.44).** The paper's own Results text (83.8 ± 6.8 %; scorer SD 7.4) differs slightly from its abstract/Table 4 (83.5 ± 6.4; SD 8.0); the notes mix the two (83.5 with 7.4). Not consequential.
- **F. DOD "5 technologists from 3 centres" (l.197).** The arXiv abstract says "from different sleep centers"; the count of three was not found on the pages read.
- **G. "ZMax 832 vs 467 REM events" (l.47, l.62).** These are pooled means with very large SDs (831.57 ± 549 vs 467.01 ± 512) from an automatic REM detector run on both signals; the direction (over-detection on ZMax) is solid, the magnitude is not a stable ratio.
- **H. ezscore-f cross-device result (l.50).** Two nights, but the preprint is inconsistent on whether they come from one or two participants; either way it is a single-digit-night, single-device transfer test and should not be generalised.
- **I. The "no public Muse sleep dataset" claim (row 18).** Correct for Zenodo, PhysioNet, OpenNeuro and web search on 2026-10-21, but the sibling verification `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` shows open GitHub *code* for Muse overnight staging exists; the two statements are compatible (code without shared data) and the report must not merge them into "nothing open exists for Muse sleep".
