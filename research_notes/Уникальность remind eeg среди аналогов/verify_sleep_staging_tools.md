## Claims that are technically true but misleading in context

A. **Mehdi et al. 2026 "350-subject subsample" (l.21, l.201).** True, but the 350 are split 270 train / 30 validation / 50 test; YASA's zero-shot REM F1 0.5531 and all fine-tuned numbers are measured on **50 test subjects**. Say "evaluated on 50 MESA test subjects (350-subject subsample)".

B. **Parry & Briganti OK-group numbers (l.20, l.99).** Accuracy 60.0 %, REM recall 0.62 / 0.44 and U-Sleep κ 0.761 are for the OK-signal subgroup (N = 71–74), not all 100 participants; the all-subject κ values (0.400 YASA, 0.349 Dreamento) are the figures that describe unscreened home use.

C. **ezscore-f cross-device κ 0.65 (l.104, l.129, l.182).** The notes say "2 nights" but omit that both nights are from **one participant** and that the device was the JHU/APL DCM patch (the table wrongly says CGX; see row 8). This is a single-subject demonstration, not a validation.

D. **OSSMM "macro-F1 0.770, accuracy 0.776, κ 0.63 vs PSG" (l.116).** Only κ 0.63 is vs PSG; the macro-F1 0.770 / accuracy 0.776 were measured **against a validated non-contact sleep monitor**, on 15 nights of one participant. The abstract also says "All hardware designs, software, and build instructions are openly available" (no URL given), so "open-source" is claimed but the repository was not located.

E. **Portiloop "precision/recall ≈ 71 %, ≈ 300 ms" (l.69, l.119, l.151).** Confirmed (PMC9394839), but 0.71/0.71 is at the chosen real-time threshold (0.84); the whole-cohort per-data-point result is recall 0.64, precision 0.59, F1 0.61; total latency is ~314 ms (64 ms constant + ~250 ± 100 ms detection). portiloop-software has 6 stars and no license file (GitHub API), which the notes correctly report as "not shown".

F. **Dreamento as a real-time precedent (l.68, l.141).** True that it updates every 30 s, but the repo has not been pushed since 2024-03-18 and the real-time TinySleepNet model was trained on **one person's** ZMax+PSG nights with no published agreement figures (the notes say this in Gaps l.135; the table and KQ3 takeaway should carry the caveat too).

G. **"No general open stager offers a streaming API" (l.72).** True for YASA, U-Sleep, Luna POPS, SleepTransformer and the DeepSleepNet family, but a reader should not extend it to "no open real-time sleep-state detection on Muse": EazzZyLearn_pc (row 15) does online sleep/wake + SWS detection and cue timing on Muse hardware.

H. **SleepFM "Nature Medicine 2026" (l.38, l.64).** The DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-04133-4 indicates online publication in 2025; the README's BibTeX says 2026. Cite as "Thapa et al., Nat Med (online 2025; vol. 2026)".
