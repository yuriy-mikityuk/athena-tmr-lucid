## 1. PSG-anchored external validation of the REM detector on open headband+PSG data, with the ocular-feature redesign

What to build or do (issue-sized items):
1. A `muse_tmr.data.external` loader that reads BOAS (OpenNeuro ds005555, CC0, BIDS, 128 nights from 100 participants, Bitbrain Ikon headband AF7/AF8 at 256 Hz plus 6 IMU channels and a PPG pulse channel, with consensus-scored PSG from three AASM scorers and a fourth tie-breaker) into the existing `MuseFrame` stream as an EEG+IMU+PPG source with TP9/TP10 absent (missing modalities are already normal in the pipeline). Consensus `stage_hum` becomes the reference hypnogram; `stage_ai` is kept as a secondary column.
2. Land the #116 fix as a separate PR: replace `_eye_movement_proxy()` with a low-passed (≤5 Hz) bipolar AF7−AF8 event detector (amplitude/duration criteria, REM count and density per epoch, per-night percentile normalisation), zero the old weight, keep the old column for replay comparability.
3. A batch harness (`muse-tmr validate-external --dataset boas`) that runs heuristic detector, personal classifier and `StableRemGate` across all 128 nights with subject-level splits (train/fit on one subject set, report on held-out subjects; never intra-subject) and reports per-night and pooled REM precision/recall/F1/κ for `P_REM` thresholds, gate-open precision ("when the gate opens, how often is PSG REM"), REM-onset-to-gate-open latency distribution, false-open minutes per night, and per-feature AUC — the same table #116 produced against GSSC, now against PSG consensus.
4. Refit detector thresholds and the gate grid on BOAS held-out folds instead of on the single Muse night; store the fitted preset as `boas_v1` next to `p21`, and record in `presets.py` that `p21` remains "tuned on one night vs GSSC".
5. Commit the derived metrics (JSON, per-night CSV of 30-s labels and gate states — no raw EEG) under `data/validation_public/boas/`; BOAS is CC0 so derived labels are shareable.
6. Optional second dataset: Wearanize+ (ZMax F7-Fpz/F8-Fpz at 256 Hz, 100 PlugNPlay participants, EEG-BIDS 1.10, Radboud DUA) as the cross-device test, mirroring ezscore-f's protocol.
7. In parallel, seek one or more Muse S Athena + PSG nights (the author's own night in a sleep lab, or a collaboration; Lanthier's Ottawa group holds 47 Muse-S+PSG nights "available on reasonable request"). Even one night converts the BOAS result from "montage analogue" to "this device".

Evidence: BOAS facts — `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` row 14 and `verify_wearable_rem_physiology_open_science.md` row 12 (CC0, 128 subject folders, v1.1.3 2026-08-31, scoring paper Esparza-Iaizzo et al., Comput Biol Med 2026;205:111560); BOAS as the closest open proxy to Muse's frontal channels — `sleep_staging_tools.md` KQ5, `wearable_rem_physiology_open_science.md` KQ7. Device-specific training matters more than architecture: DreamentoScorer κ 0.72 vs vendor ZLab 0.60 on ZMax; ezscore-f real-time model κ 0.71–0.73 with REM per-stage accuracy 91.3 %; generic YASA on home ZMax κ 0.45, REM recall 0.62 — `wearable…` KQ2, `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` rows 3, 6, 8. Frontal headbands over-call REM (Muse-S +6.4 min; Dreem +7.9 min young, ~25 min older adults; ZMax ≈832 vs 467 REM events) so gating must require sustained evidence — `wearable…` KQ1/KQ2, `verify_wearable…` rows 1, 6, S8. Intra-subject evaluation inflates agreement; subject-level splits and zero-shot transfer are the field's requirement — `wearable…` KQ4/KQ8. The ocular-feature diagnosis and the ZMax precedent for frontal REM-event detection (r = 0.66 counts, 0.60 density vs PSG EOG, systematic over-counting) — `gap_4…` KQ A/B. The "effectively 1-feature" detector and the n=1 blocker — `repo_inventory.md` KQ3, `gap_2…` A2.

Why no comparable open project has it: ezscore-f (`coonwg1/ezscore`, MIT, preprint) is validated on ZMax but is a stager, not a cueing engine, was untestable in an independent 2026 evaluation because of installation incompatibilities, and has never been run on Muse — `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` row 8, `verify_wearable…` rows 7–8. `southsko/muse-sleep-dashboard` applies YASA to Muse with no validation; `EazzZyLearn_pc` quotes a non-Muse, unpublished 85.88 % SWS figure from a code comment; Dreamento's real-time scorer was trained on one person's nights with no published agreement — `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` row 15 and item F, `gap_1…` §2. No open REM-gated system reports REM agreement against PSG consensus for its own detector.

Effort: large — new dataset loader (BIDS/EDF reading, channel mapping, reference handling), feature redesign, 128-night batch runs, threshold regrid, documentation; the Muse+PSG night depends on an external lab.

Risk / caveat: BOAS is Ikon AF7/AF8 referenced to the mastoids; Muse references near Fpz, which attenuates horizontal eye-movement amplitude and shifts band powers (ZMax's active Fpz reference underestimates band power; a per-subject calibration removes the bias — `wearable…` KQ2). Thresholds will therefore need per-night normalisation and a Muse re-check; cross-device transfer evidence is thin (ezscore-f's DCM κ 0.65 rests on two nights from one participant — `verify_sleep_staging_tools.md` item C). A BOAS result bounds the method on a frontal montage, not Muse; the Muse+PSG night remains n = 1. Nothing here speaks to cueing efficacy.

## 4. Standardised post-cue arousal scoring and safety outcomes

What to build or do:
1. `muse_tmr.reports.cue_arousal` (retrospective, replay-based; `ArousalGuard` remains the prospective component): for each scheduler `play`, score a 10-s post-cue window against a 10-s pre-cue baseline for frontal alpha (8–12 Hz) and beta (15–30 Hz) relative-power rise, a high-frequency EMG-band proxy (>40 Hz, with the 64-Hz optics line notched or excluded on `p1034`), IMU movement, and PPG heart-rate jump; label each flag as a proxy, not an AASM arousal.
2. Matched silent control windows: for every cue, sample a window in the same gate state with no cue within ±60 s, and report flags per cue vs per silent window (Konkoly 2024's 0.19 vs 0.10 movement events design).
3. Outcomes added to `pilot4_summary.json` and `docs/validation.md` as safety metrics with their own table: arousal-proxy-per-cue rate (by flag type), cue-attributed awakening rate (join with `awakening_events.jsonl`), fraction of REM periods with any disruption (Dresler 2024 reports 23.7 %), REM-period termination within 60 s of a cue, and dose–response tables by effective volume, inter-cue interval and cue index within block.
4. Replay regression: a Pilot 3 variant that injects synthetic cue events into a replayed night and asserts the scorer's windows and controls are placed correctly; a dry-run night produces an empty but valid report.
5. A one-page `docs/safety_outcomes.md` stating the benchmark values from the literature next to the repo's own definitions, so a night can be read as "x % arousal proxies per cue vs 43–58 % after lab cues tuned to arouse".

Evidence: cues followed by arousals convert memory benefit into forgetting — Whitmore & Paller 2023: arousals after 57.9 % of loud (≈43 dBA) and 43.0 % of soft (≈28 dBA, 31.7 % of initial arousal threshold) cues, cued-with-arousal items forgotten more (t(22) = 3.28, p = .003) — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ4 with corrected figures in `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` row 9; cue-evoked alpha in the 5 s after a cue predicts lost benefit (Whitmore, Bassard & Paller 2022) and the home SleepStim system lowered its intensity set-point when p(N3) dropped during sounds, turning a null (N = 61) into a benefit (N = 24, z = 2.69) — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ4, `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` row 10; home TLR cues raised movement events per cue 0.19 vs 0.10 (p = .002) and "often woke participants" — Konkoly et al. 2024, `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ3/KQ4; multisensory REM cues disrupted sleep in 23.7 % of cued REM periods — Picard-Deland et al. 2024 preprint (v2 2026), `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` row 8; Coon et al. 2026 design review: log stimuli and arousals, stricter settings in REM, forehead EEG is artifact-prone so use PPG/accelerometry — `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` spot check; Philips SmartSleep used 15–30 Hz power to detect micro-arousals (age-group claim dropped) — `wearable…` KQ6, `verify_wearable…` S10; tonic REM has lower awakening thresholds than phasic — `wearable…` KQ6. The repo's `validation.md` has guard-burden counts and "cue-window motion count" but no per-cue EEG arousal metric or control-window comparison — `docs/validation.md` lines 41–53 (checked).

Why no comparable open project has it: `nathanww/home-tmr` logs arousal-triggered backoff from watch data only and has been dormant since 2022-12; COsleep disengages on arousal and logs markers but publishes no per-cue arousal statistics and is stale (2023-07); SMACC's event log is experimenter-driven; EazzZyLearn has no arousal input beyond band-ratio wake staging — `cueing_tools_landscape.md` KQ1/KQ4, `gap_1…` §4. Published systems report arousal-per-cue only inside papers; no open engine computes it from its own logs with matched silent controls.

Effort: medium — features (relative alpha, stillness, HR) already exist per epoch; the work is sub-epoch windowing around events, control sampling, joins with awakening logs, report tables and tests.

Risk / caveat: frontal-only EEG lacks occipital alpha and chin EMG, which "limits arousal detection and Wake vs N1 distinction" (Esfahani et al. 2024, `wearable…` KQ3); AASM arousal scoring needs an abrupt EEG frequency shift ≥3 s with concurrent EMG rise in REM, so all outputs are proxies with no validation against PSG-scored arousals; no human study validates TP9/TP10 high-frequency power as an atonia substitute (`wearable…` KQ3 gaps). Volume is a software gain (0–1), not dB SPL, so dose–response is relative to the user's calibration, not comparable across devices.

## 5. Night-level sham and no-cue arms, N-of-1 block randomisation, SCRIBE/CENT-style reporting

What to build or do:
1. `protocol/night_schedule.py`: a seeded generator of randomised night blocks with arms `cue`, `sham` (an untrained sound at the same calibrated volume through the identical gate, guard and scheduler path), and `no_cue` (gate and scheduler run, playback suppressed), with washout nights, counterbalanced block order, and a hash commitment of the sequence written at generation time and revealed only by an explicit unblinding command (the current item-level randomiser in `randomization.py` stays as the within-night layer).
2. Scheduler and audio changes: an `arm` field in every scheduler and audio JSONL event; sham mode selects the untrained cue from the cue library; `ensure_schedulable()` logic extended so a `no_cue` night cannot emit `play`; Pilot 3/4 validators gain a check that the executed arm matches the sealed schedule.
3. Morning flow stays blind: the app's Morning tab must not reveal the arm (it already hides `cue_condition`); the dream report gains "did a sound wake you" and a cue-perception item, both arm-blind.
4. `reports/nof1_analysis.py`: per-arm descriptive outcomes (lucid report rate, cues-heard rate, arousal-proxy rate from initiative 4, cued-vs-uncued solve delta on cue nights) with night-to-night autocorrelation and first-night/habituation flags, a simple Bayesian or permutation estimate with an interval, and an explicit statement that inference is single-case; a `limitations` list extended with `no_sham_nights`, `unbalanced_arms`, `unblinded_early`.
5. A SCRIBE 2016 / CENT 2015 item checklist rendered into the Markdown report (randomisation method, blinding, sequence, adherence, data loss), plus an OSF-preregistration template generated from the protocol manifest (initiative 6) and the sealed schedule hash.

Evidence: the only randomised, participant-blind home TLR design used untrained-sound and no-cue control nights interleaved with TLR nights (Konkoly et al. 2024 Exp 2: interaction F(1,118) = 6.28, p = .01; no-cue vs TLR p = .77) — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ3/KQ6, `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` row 7; the preregistered multi-centre study found SVLD rates 38.3 % stim vs 28.3 % sham, not different, while cues entered dreams in 71.1 % of cued REM periods — `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` row 8; Konkoly et al. 2026 list "no no-cue night" and incomplete blinding as limitations — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ2; Lustenberger 2022 used blinded sham nights with recorded triggers and no sound — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ6; N-of-1 methodology (Schmid & Yang 2022; Konigorski, Ried-Larsen & Schmid 2026; SCRIBE/CENT) and the absence of any N-of-1 TMR/TLR study — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ7; Göldi & Rasch 2019 found a whole-sample benefit only on night 3, so first-night effects must be modelled — `tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ1; Hu 2020's "unspecified-stage" null (g = 0.26, k = 6) is consistent with, but does not demonstrate, an EEG-gating advantage — `verify_tmr_tlr_science.md` misleading item 1.

Why no comparable open project has it: COsleep has sham/stim condition obfuscation only (OpenBCI, Linux, stale); `Taran-Douley/closed-loop-sleep` lists "randomised sham vs real nights" as unimplemented Phase 2; `occult-kranti/brainwave_opensync` offers seeded, blinded n-of-1 blocks but measures no physiology; home-tmr and Dreamento have no randomisation code — `cueing_tools_landscape.md` KQ4, `verify_cueing_tools_landscape.md` misleading item 3 (state "none of the repositories inspected", not "none exists"), `gap_1…` §5/§9. No open sleep-cueing engine combines physiology-gated cueing with sealed night-level arms and single-case reporting.

Effort: medium — generator, scheduler/audio fields, validator checks, analysis, report template, tests; no new signal processing.

Risk / caveat: a sham arm still delivers sound, so arousal confounds are only partly controlled, and expectation effects remain (the user knows a protocol is running); self-reported lucidity and recall are the outcomes; n = 1 estimates are single-case descriptions with intervals, never population effects; no power heuristics exist for N-of-1 sleep cueing (`tmr_tlr_science.md` KQ7 gaps). Preregistration of a self-experiment documents intent; it does not make the result generalisable.

## What the repository should not try to compete on

Raw Athena BLE streaming, LSL bridging and live visualisation are solved by larger, multi-OS-tested projects (muse-lsl, BrainFlow, OpenMuse, muse-rs, BlueMuse; `verify_muse_ecosystem.md` rows 1–8); the right move is to keep the OpenMuse/BrainFlow adapters current and let the forked amused-py layer shrink rather than grow. fNIRS HbO/HbR derivation from Athena optics is declined by every maintainer for lack of validated wavelength and source–detector geometry, and no 2021–2026 study detects REM or arousals from fNIRS (`wearable…` KQ5, `verify_wearable…` S3); log raw optics, do not claim haemodynamics. NREM slow-oscillation phase-locked CLAS is a different science with sub-150-ms latency needs and an existing open field (EazzZyLearn_pc, Taran-Douley, COsleep, Portiloop, Dreem/SleepLoop validations); the 30-s-epoch architecture is not built for it. General-purpose sleep staging models cannot be out-trained by one author against YASA, U-Sleep, ezscore-f or SleepFM; use them as references (initiative 2) and keep the in-house detector small and explainable. Automated LRLR eye-signal verification of lucidity from four frontal channels has no validated algorithm anywhere in the 2021–Oct 2026 literature — every study used human raters with majority voting (`gap_4…` KQ B); at most ship "LRLR-ready" logging and manual marking. Consumer features (REM-timed alarms such as Dream Recall, sleep scores, app polish), cross-platform launchers, cloud sync and multi-user accounts add no scientific weight and the last two conflict with the repository's data policy. Finally, the repository should not compete on efficacy claims of any kind — lucid-dream induction, memory or nightmare outcomes — because the best-controlled studies remain modest or null (Hu 2020 REM g = −0.06; Dresler 2024 induction vs sham n.s.; Konkoly 2026 no overall cueing effect) and REMind's own distinctive value is auditability, protocol fidelity and measured safety, not effect size.
