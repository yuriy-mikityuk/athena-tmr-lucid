# REM and sleep micro-state detection on consumer forehead wearables (2021–Oct 2026): physiology, PSG validation, open data and open-science infrastructure

Scope note: all findings below come from web search + full-text fetches (Elicit API was unavailable to this account). Peer-reviewed, preprint and vendor sources are labelled. "Muse-S" below is the pre-Athena Muse S (Gen 2); nothing peer-reviewed on Muse S Athena's fNIRS for sleep was found.

## Key Question 1 — Muse S / Muse S Athena sleep validation against PSG (agreement, kappa, REM)

### Takeaway
One independent peer-reviewed lab study (Lanthier et al., SLEEP Advances 2025, N=56, 47 analysed) found Muse-S + InteraXon's "sleep staging v2" reaches full-night Cohen's κ = 0.76 with REM κ = 0.85, REM sensitivity 91.5% / specificity 96.8%, and a +6.4 min REM bias — the highest REM agreement reported for any consumer forehead headband I found. Everything about Athena-specific sleep performance and its fNIRS/SpO2 contribution is vendor-claimed only.

### Cited Findings

- Baseline (pre-2021, different device): a 2013 PSG validation of a wireless dry headband (N=11) reported κ light 0.56, deep 0.70, REM 0.67 — [SRI publication](https://www.sri.com/publication/biosciences-health-pubs/polysomnographic-validation-of-a-wireless-dry-headband-technology-for-sleep-monitoring-in-healthy-young-adults/)

### Inferences
- The only independent PSG evidence is for Muse-S hardware with InteraXon's closed algorithm; REMind, which must run its own open REM detector on the same four frontal/temporal channels, cannot inherit the κ 0.85 REM figure and should treat it as the ceiling to benchmark against.
- A 16% unusable-night rate even with technician fitting in a lab implies REMind needs a pre-sleep signal-quality gate and must expect higher loss at home.
- Lanthier's REM overestimation (+6.4 min) and ezscore-f's/Dreamento's early-REM tendency (see Q2/Q3) all point in the same direction: frontal-only algorithms tend to call REM early/long, which is a false-positive risk for cue triggering.
- The 16-bit EEG, Fpz-referenced frontal montage means REMind's REM features are, in practice, EOG-contaminated frontal EEG — which is a feature for REM detection (see Q3), not only an artifact.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or preprint validation of Muse S Athena (as opposed to Muse-S) sleep staging, and none of Athena fNIRS or SpO2 for sleep; the BusinessWire release could not be fetched (403) so its "retrospective and real-time" validation wording is unverified.
- Whether IEEE document 10123829 is the Abou Jaoude NER 2023 paper, and its per-stage REM numbers, could not be confirmed.
- No published latency/real-time behaviour of Muse's own staging, and no public Muse sleep dataset.

## Key Question 2 — Other consumer forehead EEG headbands validated against PSG (Dreem, ZMax, Bitbrain, FRENZ, Elemind) and the algorithms used

### Takeaway
Across forehead headbands the pattern is stable: with device-specific trained models, REM is one of the best-detected stages (Dreem REM κ 0.79 / F1 0.83; ZMax+DreamentoScorer REM F1 0.83; ezscore-f REM accuracy ~0.89–0.91; Muse-S REM κ 0.85), while N1 and Wake are the weak points — but generic PSG-trained scorers applied to forehead EEG collapse (YASA κ 0.45, Dreamento κ 0.37 on home ZMax), and 7–16% of home nights are lost to signal dropout.

### Cited Findings

Company-affiliated authors — [PMC7751170](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7751170)
- Peer-reviewed: Ravindran et al., Sleep 2025, DOI 10.1093/sleep/zsaf122 (medRxiv 2024.12.18.24319240): Dreem headband + its algorithm vs PSG in 62 older adults (70.5±6.7 y; 12 with Alzheimer's). - Peer-reviewed (2025): forehead + in-ear EEG acquisition with a memory-efficient two-step LSTM for 5-class staging from a single frontal derivation — [PMC12526609](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12526609/)

### Inferences
- Device-specific training matters more than architecture: DreamentoScorer (LightGBM) beat the vendor's ZLab by 12 κ-points, and ezscore-f reaches REM accuracy ~0.9 on ZMax, while off-the-shelf YASA/Dreamento on a different cohort's home ZMax drop to κ 0.37–0.45. REMind's REM detector should be trained/fine-tuned on frontal-montage data (BOAS AF7/AF8 is the closest open proxy to Muse's AF7/AF8) and validated with a held-out subject split.
- Frontal headbands over-call REM (Dreem +7.9 min young, +25 min older adults; Muse +6.4 min; ZMax 832 vs 467 REM events) — a cue scheduler should require sustained REM evidence, not single-epoch calls.
- Signal-quality triage is as important as the classifier: 7–16% of nights are lost outright, and muscle contamination affects another ~16%; a pre-lights-off impedance/amplitude/muscle-band check and per-epoch artifact class (as in ezscore-f) are evidence-backed design elements.
- Reference-electrode effects (ZMax active Fpz → band-power underestimation) suggest REMind should report relative band powers and per-night normalisation rather than absolute thresholds.

### Gaps
- No PSG validation found for Elemind staging, Bitbrain Ikon as a standalone product, iBand, or Beacon-branded (post-Dreem) hardware.
- Per-stage REM sensitivity/specificity tables for ZMax (Esfahani Table 2) and ezscore-f (Fig. 6) were not readable from fetched text.
- The ZMax validation remains a 2023 preprint; no journal version was found.

## Key Question 3 — REM information in frontal EEG (EOG contamination, sawtooth waves, EMG proxies) and how closed-loop REM studies detected REM online and timed cues

### Takeaway
Frontal/frontopolar derivations carry REM information mainly through EOG pickup (REM eye movements are "discernible" in F7/F8 and AF7/AF8 channels; REM counts correlate r≈0.6–0.66 with EOG) and through REM-specific frontal sawtooth bursts; the published closed-loop REM protocols verified REM with full PSG (EOG + chin EMG) and waited anywhere from the first 30-s REM epoch (Paris) to 5 min of stable REM (Osnabrück) before cueing, pausing on any arousal, with cue volumes ~40–45 dB SPL.

### Cited Findings
- Eye movements in frontal EEG: in the ZMax validation, REM-related eye movements are "discernible within the ZMax signal" (F7–F8 compared with EOG L−R); REM counts and REM density correlated between devices (r = 0.66 and 0.60) but REM duration/speed did not — [Esfahani et al. 2023 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.08.18.553744.full)
- A PSG methods paper used frontopolar AF7/AF8–Fpz channels as EOG-like signals during REM and observed a facial-muscle twitch in frontopolar channels but not in submental EMG — [Nature and Science of Sleep 2018](https://www.dovepress.com/article/download/42869)
- Muse-S recordings were time-aligned to PSG using eye-blink sequences during biocalibration, i.e., blinks are prominent in Muse's frontal channels — [Lanthier et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12782022/)
- Sawtooth waves: high-density EEG showed frontal-central sawtooth waves occur in bursts, have a faster frequency than other REM delta activity, co-occur with rapid eye movements and increased gamma power, and are specific to REM (a separate medial-occipital slow-wave type also appears in NREM) — [Bernardi et al. 2019, J Neurosci, PubMed 30737310](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30737310/)
- Single-channel EEG REM detection is hard because REM resembles N1 and Wake spectrally; the classic single-channel approach (spectral edge frequency 8–16 Hz) reached 83% sensitivity / 89% specificity / 61% selectivity — [Imperial Spiral](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/9b48ece2-ff8c-4498-b744-1f18c6350a3a); [PMC10525287](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10525287/)
- Frontal-only montage lacks occipital alpha, which "limits arousal detection and Wake vs N1 distinction" (authors' limitation for 2-channel ZMax) — [Esfahani et al. 2024 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v1.full)
- EMG proxy from EEG: in mice, 80–500 Hz components of single-channel EEG separated REM from wake with ~98% sensitivity / ~92% specificity, with 200–500 Hz bands most predictive — [Sci Rep 2023, PMC10264428](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10264428/); a 2020 Sensors paper argues the usual EMG band is ~40–120 Hz and that >80 Hz EEG content "may carry EMG-related information" but that it is "unclear whether this high-frequency content will add additional information" to staging — [Sensors 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/7/2024); the human reference for atonia is a high-/low-frequency chin-EMG power ratio validated on 1,618,842 s of REM from 461 participants — [PMC7180982](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180982)
- Konkoly KR et al., "Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep", Current Biology 2021;31:1417–1427, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026 (peer-reviewed). REM was "verified with standard polysomnographic methods" (frontal, central, occipital EEG; two EOG; chin EMG), re-scored blind by three certified scorers; 80.4% (127/158) of communication trials were scored REM by all three — [Konkoly 2021 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/04/Konkoly_2021_Curr_Biol.pdf)
- Konkoly 2021, USA (Northwestern) procedure: TLR cue = three rising pure tones 400/600/800 Hz at ~40–45 dB SPL, ~650 ms; once participants entered REM, cues presented "at about 30-s intervals, as long as REM sleep remained stable", ~10 cues before switching to math problems — [Konkoly 2021 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/04/Konkoly_2021_Curr_Biol.pdf)
- Konkoly 2021, Germany (Osnabrück) procedure: 4.5 h after sleep onset wait for REM, wake after 10 min REM, 45 min awake (WBTB), then in each following REM period "after 5 min of stable REM sleep" present Morse-coded stimuli; intensity increased per problem "until either the participant responded with eye signals, had an arousal, or went into non-REM sleep, in which case stimulation was stopped"; 19-channel EEG + EOG + chin EMG at 500 Hz — [Konkoly 2021 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/04/Konkoly_2021_Curr_Biol.pdf)
- Konkoly 2021, France (Paris) procedure: cues "administered again after the first 30-s epoch of REM sleep, one cue every 10–15 s. Cues were paused if the EEG showed an arousal and resumed at REM-sleep onset. Up to 10 cues were presented during each REM sleep period"; auditory cues on a white-noise background capped at 45 dBA ("whisper-like") — [Konkoly 2021 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/04/Konkoly_2021_Curr_Biol.pdf)
- Preprint: Jafarzadeh Esfahani M, Salvesen LAC, Picard-Deland C, … Carr M, Dresler M. "Highly effective verified lucid dream induction using combined cognitive-sensory training and wearable EEG: a multi-centre study", bioRxiv 2024, DOI 10.1101/2024.06.21.600133 (a 2026 version is retitled "Inducing lucid dreaming with multisensory stimulation: a preregistered multi-center study"). With 2-channel ZMax + Dreamento, cueing began "about 20 seconds after the first rapid eye movement was detected alongside low EMG activity"; cues (light/sound/vibration cycle) ~every 20 s starting at individual perceptual threshold, rising +5 dBA / +5% per cycle; on signs of microarousal (relative rise in EMG or alpha) stimulation paused until resolved, then resumed one step lower; LRLR lucidity signals were detected in the frontal EEG; signal-verified lucid dreams in 65% (NL) and 45% (IT) of participants; REM-cueing vs sham: NL 45% vs 35% (n.s., p=0.26), IT 35% vs 15% (McNemar p=0.021); limitation: "automation for home use is still needed" — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v1.full)
- ZMax's built-in online REM detection starts only after a ~2-hour delay to build an individual EEG phenotype, and "not enough technical detail is public" on the algorithm — [Mota-Rolim et al. 2019, Front Neurosci, PMC6517539](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6517539/)
- Dreamento (MIT license; cite SoftwareX 2023;24:101595): real-time staging is "open to any algorithm" but "not ideal yet, still under development"; DreamentoScorer = LightGBM on time/time-frequency features, 30-s epochs, trained on >130 recordings, outputs hypnodensity; an on-by-default post-scoring rule relabels REM before the first N2 as N1 — [Dreamento GitHub](https://github.com/dreamento/dreamento); [arXiv 2207.03977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03977v2)
- Home TLR without EEG (peer-reviewed): Konkoly KR, Whitmore NW, Mallett R, Mazurek CY, Paller KA. "Provoking lucid dreams at home with sensory cues paired with pre-sleep cognitive training", Consciousness and Cognition 2024;125:103759. No staging; cueing started 6 h after training; cues every 20–40 s (Exp 1) at discrimination threshold rising 0.16% per cue until awakening; phone accelerometer paused cueing for 5 min (Exp 1) / 1 min (Exp 2) after movement; lucid dreams rose from 0.74/week to 2.11 (Exp 1, n=19, p=.007); Exp 2 (n=112 first two nights) night-2 group difference OR 3.61, p=.006; "cues often woke participants … like an alarm"; motion after TLR cues 0.19/cue vs 0.10 after no cue (p=.002); authors could not rule out that cues worked by increasing arousal — [PMC11542932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11542932)
- Real-time latency benchmark from a different oscillation: Portiloop detected sleep spindles with 71% precision/recall and stimulated within ~300 ms of onset on open hardware — [Valenchon et al. 2022 PLoS ONE, PMC9394839](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9394839/)

### Inferences
- For REMind, the "EOG contamination" of AF7/AF8 is the main REM channel: REM-burst detection (bilateral out-of-phase deflections at AF7 vs AF8, REM density) plus frontal sawtooth-like 2–5 Hz bursts are the physiologically grounded frontal REM markers; TP9/TP10 high-frequency power as an atonia proxy is plausible but unvalidated in humans.
- A conservative, evidence-aligned REM gate would mirror the lab protocols: at least one full 30-s epoch scored REM (Paris), preferably several minutes of stable REM (Osnabrück's 5 min) or first REM burst + low muscle activity + ~20 s (Dresler lab), with cues at ≥10–30 s intervals and a hard stop on any arousal/NREM transition.
- Published systems never let a single-epoch REM probability trigger audio; all used stability criteria plus arousal pause rules — consistent with REMind's separate gate + arousal guard architecture.

### Gaps
- No human study validating temporal/mastoid EEG channels (TP9/TP10) as a chin-EMG substitute for atonia.
- No published onset-to-detection latency for any consumer wearable's real-time REM detector (ZMax's 2-h warm-up is the only timing fact found).
- The Dutch group's cue/REM criteria in Konkoly 2021 were not extracted (procedure text truncated).

## Key Question 4 — REM from PPG/HRV (and PPG+accelerometer) and whether fusing PPG with frontal EEG helps

### Takeaway
PPG(+accelerometer)-only staging reaches κ ≈ 0.55–0.75 overall, with REM sensitivity 67–83% on wrist devices in a 35-person lab study (Oura 76%, Apple Watch 83%, Fitbit 67%) and REM F1 ≈ 0.63 for an open smartwatch model — useful corroboration but far below frontal-EEG REM κ 0.79–0.85; the only EEG+PPG fusion paper found (2026 preprint, central EEG) gained mainly in light sleep, not REM, so no evidence yet that PPG improves REM detection on a headband.

### Cited Findings
- Peer-reviewed, Oura-funded: Robbins R et al. "Accuracy of Three Commercial Wearable Devices for Sleep Tracking in Healthy Adults", Sensors 2024;24(20):6532, DOI 10.3390/s24206532. N=35 (20–50 y), single inpatient PSG night; 4-stage κ: Oura Ring Gen3 0.65, Apple Watch Series 8 0.60, Fitbit Sense 2 0.55. REM sensitivity / precision: Oura 76.0% / 79.1%; Apple Watch 82.6% / 77.7%; Fitbit 67.3% / 73.1%; nightly REM ICC vs PSG only 0.13–0.37; deep-sleep sensitivity Oura 79.5%, Fitbit 61.7%, Apple 50.5%; first author on Oura advisory board — [PMC11511193](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmc11511193)
- Peer-reviewed: Kotzen K et al., SleepPPG-Net, IEEE JBHI 2023;27(2):924–932 (arXiv 2202.05735): end-to-end on raw PPG downsampled to ≈34 Hz, 1,024 samples per 30-s window, 1,200-window sequences; MESA test (n=204) median κ 0.75, accuracy 84%; CFS (n=320) κ 0.67 → 0.74 with transfer learning; "deep sleep is consistently underestimated and often confused with light sleep"; REM fraction MSE 4.08%; no REM-specific error analysis — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.05735)
- Preprint: SleepPPG-Net2 (arXiv 2404.06869, 2024) — multi-source domain training improved generalisation κ by up to 19% and found performance disparities by age, sex and apnea severity — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2404.06869)
- Google Research 2024 (Gillard, Heneghan, Niehaus): CNN on raw green PPG + 3-D accelerometer at 25 Hz, 4 classes; 2-stage accuracy 0.94 / κ 0.79; **4-stage accuracy 0.79 / κ 0.66** on a 16-record test set — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/sleep-staging-classification-from-wearable-signals-using-deep-learning/)
- WatchSleepNet (2025, wrist PPG): REM F1 0.631±0.046, κ 0.554±0.027 — [Duke CompHealth](https://comphealth.duke.edu/publications/5474-2/)
- Peer-reviewed (Sci Rep, Jun 2023): neural network on wrist PPG inter-beat intervals + accelerometry, median κ 0.638, accuracy 77.8%, ~50× faster than HRV-feature approach — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1d9458e787b04caf95576ccd6305c8e1)
- Peer-reviewed: wrist PPG classifier in 244 adults + 48 children with sleep disorders: 4-class κ 0.62; weaker in REM parasomnias — [PubMed 34234595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34234595/)
- An ECG-trained HRV staging algorithm performed worse on wrist-PPG-derived HRV (κ 0.56 vs 0.60; 389 PSGs) — PPG models need their own validation — [PMC7653690](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7653690)
- Camera rPPG (HealthBed 2023, N=46): κ 0.58 (3-class), 0.49 (4-class) — [PMC9855193](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9855193/)
- Preprint (arXiv 2602.15042, 2026): single-channel EEG + PPG fusion (score-level, cross-attention, Mamba) on MESA: best κ 0.798, accuracy 86.9%; gains concentrated in light sleep (F1 85.6% vs 77.8% for EEG alone); authors note "PPG-based sleep staging methods generally achieve lower overall accuracy than scEEG-based approaches due to the indirect nature of autonomic signals" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15042)
- PPG pulse-wave feature fusion (ETASR 2025): 77.8% for wake/NREM/REM, 69.2% for 4-stage — [ETASR](https://www.etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/13077)
- Preprint 2026 systematic review (not peer-reviewed): deep, REM and N1 are consistently the hardest stages for wearables; intra-subject evaluation inflates agreement; zero-shot transfer to structurally different consumer wearables remains unsolved — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202607.1006)
- Dreem's headband already integrated frontal PPG and accelerometer alongside EEG (Arnal 2020), and Bitbrain Ikon and ZMax Lite ship PPG + IMU at 256 Hz — [PMC7751170](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7751170); [Wearanize+](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12888818/)

### Inferences
- On Athena, forehead PPG is best used as (a) an independent REM/arousal corroborator (REM is autonomically distinct, but wrist devices still miss 17–33% of REM epochs), (b) an HR-surge arousal guard, and (c) a fallback when EEG quality fails — not as the primary REM trigger.
- Because the one fusion study improved light sleep rather than REM, REMind should not expect PPG to fix REM false positives; it may help the Wake/N1-vs-REM confusion indirectly via HR/HRV wake signatures.
- Datasets with headband EEG + PPG + PSG (BOAS, Wearanize+) make it feasible to test EEG+PPG fusion for REM specifically — nobody appears to have published that yet.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of forehead (as opposed to wrist/finger) PPG for staging, and none showing PPG fusion improves REM detection on a headband.
- Direct source on REM-specific HRV physiology (e.g., LF/HF shifts, phasic-REM HR surges) was not retrieved before the search budget ran out.
- No peer-reviewed Fitbit/Google 2025 staging paper was found beyond the 2024 Google Research summary.

## Key Question 5 — fNIRS/cerebral oxygenation across sleep stages and REM; realism of using Athena fNIRS for REM or arousal detection

### Takeaway
The only stage-resolved prefrontal fNIRS findings are small or old (n=9 in 2019: REM shows lower HbO/HbT than other stages; 2011 NIRS: REM onset with eye movements transiently activates PFC), 2025 forehead-NIRS sleep devices still stage sleep exclusively from EEG/EOG, and no 2021–2026 study was found that detects REM or arousals from fNIRS alone — so Athena fNIRS is a research exploratory signal, not a validated REM detector.

### Cited Findings
- Baseline (peer-reviewed, 2019): Oniz et al., "Sleep Is a Refreshing Process: An fNIRS Study", Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2019.00160. Nine healthy adults with PSG + prefrontal fNIRS across the night: REM had lower oxyhemoglobin (HbO) and total hemoglobin (HbT) than other stages and wakefulness; deoxyhemoglobin did not differ by stage; prefrontal oxygenation higher at the end of sleep than at the start — [PMC6527902](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6527902/)
- Baseline (2011, PLOS ONE): 30 all-night NIRS + PSG recordings over right PFC; spontaneous hemodynamic oscillations differ by stage and at transitions; REM onset "accompanied by rapid eye movements which have been reported to activate the prefrontal cortex" — [PLOS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0025415](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0025415)
- Preprint (bioRxiv 2025, not peer-reviewed): a modified dual-wavelength forehead fNIRS tracking hemoglobin and water during overnight PSG found the strongest oscillatory changes in slow-wave sleep; low-frequency oscillations showed no significant differences across N2, N3, REM or Wake — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.10.27.684357](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.27.684357v1.full)
- Peer-reviewed (Science Advances): a soft wireless forehead NIRS system for brain-water (glymphatic-linked) dynamics during sleep — sleep staging "was performed exclusively using EEG and EOG signals", and the NIRS signal "was not included as an input feature in any of the classification models" — [Science Advances 10.1126/sciadv.aed2056](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aed2056)
- Conference (SPIE 2024): peripheral NIRS + fMRI + EEG showed brain–periphery hemodynamic coupling is strong in most stages but weakens in NREM3 — [SPIE 10.1117/12.3037260](https://journals.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13242/1324214/NREM3-sleep-disrupts-the-brain-periphery-hemodynamic-coupling/10.1117/12.3037260.full)
- Peer-reviewed (2021): Arif et al., fNIRS passive BCI over right DLPFC in 13 subjects for drowsiness detection with sleep-stage-based thresholds; best ensemble 95.3% — but this is drowsiness/onset, not overnight staging — [Front Hum Neurosci 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.658444/text)
- Peer-reviewed (2023, infant NIRS-EEG methods): "measuring some EEG channels is indispensable at the moment for determining sleep and sleep stages" — [PMC10519625](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10519625/)
- Earlier connectivity work (thesis, 5 volunteers): NIRS+EEG connectivity highest in N1; prefrontal connectivity differed between wake and N1/N2/REM — [Flinders thesis](https://theses.flinders.edu.au/view/286cec70-12fb-4820-8f72-95716cfc4e07/1)
- Wearanize+ participants also wore an "fNIRS headband" overnight, but those data "are not included in the release" — [Sikder et al. 2025, PMC12888818](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12888818/)
- Athena fNIRS = 5 optodes bilateral frontal at 64 Hz; the open OpenMuse driver's fNIRS extraction method is still listed as "to validate" — [OpenMuse GitHub](https://github.com/DominiqueMakowski/OpenMuse)
- Vendor: Muse ties Athena fNIRS to focus/endurance training, not to sleep staging — [Fitt Insider press release](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/muses-ai-platform-delivers-clinical-grade-sleep-and-brain-health-intelligence/)

### Inferences
- The physiologically expected REM signature on forehead fNIRS is a modest HbO/HbT decrease relative to NREM with phasic increases around eye-movement bursts — a slow (tens of seconds), low-SNR signal unsuitable as a primary or low-latency REM trigger.
- Realistic REMind uses for Athena optics: log raw optical channels for offline exploratory analysis (publishable as novel data since no open consumer fNIRS sleep dataset exists), derive SpO2/HR, and possibly use abrupt hemodynamic shifts as a secondary arousal indicator — all clearly flagged as unvalidated.

### Gaps
- No 2021–2026 peer-reviewed study of fNIRS-based REM or arousal detection was found; a NeuroImage 2025 article "Sleep indicators and staging: a fNIRS study in healthy young adults" exists but could not be accessed (HTTP 403), so its content is unknown — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811925003714)
- Athena fNIRS emitter wavelengths and source–detector distances were not found in any public source.

## Key Question 6 — Sleep micro-events relevant to safe cueing: arousal detection, phasic vs tonic REM, REM density, responses to sound, real-time arousal guards

### Takeaway
Deployed closed-loop systems use simple, conservative arousal guards — Philips SmartSleep (15–30 Hz EEG power → delay/stop), Paris and Nijmegen lucid-dream labs (EEG alpha/EMG rise → pause, resume lower), Konkoly's home app (accelerometer → 1–5 min pause) — and REM microstructure matters because tonic REM has lower awakening thresholds and stronger auditory evoked responses than phasic REM, while phasic/tonic classification is still done from EOG, not from consumer EEG.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor whitepaper (Philips SmartSleep): frontal EEG; "EEG power in the 15–30 Hz band is used to spot micro-arousals and estimate the risk that a sound will disturb sleep"; an arousal delays the next stimulation and ongoing stimulation stops if an arousal is detected; 50-ms tones at 1-s intervals with volume adjusted to sleep depth; 27-participant 10-night home pilot showed increased SWA vs sham; effect present in younger, absent in older group — [Philips whitepaper PDF](https://images.philips.com/is/content/PhilipsConsumer/Campaigns/HC20140401_DG/Documents/smartsleep-whitepaper.pdf)
- Peer-reviewed (2018): ambulatory dry-EEG headband at home — real-time N3 detection sensitivity 0.70, specificity 0.90 — [Front Hum Neurosci 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00088/pdf)
- ETH Zurich SleepLoop (home CLAS headband): triggers a click when slow waves appear; crossover trial in older adults showed robust SWA increases with large between-participant and between-night variability; "in-home auditory stimulation is feasible" — [ScienceDaily 2022](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/04/220408083849.htm); [ETH summary](https://ethrat.mironet.ch/research/enhancing-deep-sleep.html)
- Lab REM-cueing arousal rules: Paris — pause cues on EEG arousal, resume at REM onset, ≤10 cues per REM period, ≤45 dBA background; Osnabrück — stop on arousal or NREM; USA — cue only "as long as REM remained stable" — [Konkoly 2021 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/04/Konkoly_2021_Curr_Biol.pdf)
- Nijmegen/Lucca 2024 protocol: pause on "relative rise in EMG or alpha activity" until fully resolved; resume one intensity step lower; +5 dBA per cycle from individual threshold; some participants found cues disruptive, "cueing may only help when sleep is stable" — [bioRxiv 2024.06.21.600133](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v1.full)
- Home app without EEG: cues "often woke participants", motion after TLR cues 0.19 vs 0.10 per cue (p=.002); intensity hard to set because of unknown home noise and stage-dependent arousal thresholds — [Konkoly et al. 2024, PMC11542932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11542932)
- Peer-reviewed review: Simor P et al., "The microstructure of REM sleep: Why phasic and tonic?", Sleep Medicine Reviews 2020;52:101305 — tonic REM has lower awakening thresholds than phasic (Ermis et al. 2010), stronger evoked responses to infrequent sounds (Sallinen et al. 1996), residual auditory-cortex activation in tonic but not phasic REM (Wehrle et al. 2007); hd-EEG (n=18) shows higher high-alpha and beta power frontocentrally in tonic REM (Simor et al. 2018) — [ULB repository PDF](https://dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/303682/3/1-s2.0-S1087079220300484-main.pdf)
- Peer-reviewed review (2023): Barbato, "Is REM Density a Measure of Arousal during Sleep?" — REM density rises across the night as sleep pressure falls, is higher at the circadian arousal peak, drops after sleep deprivation, and is elevated in affective disorders — [PMC10046235](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10046235/); scoping review of REM-density methods — [MDPI Clocks & Sleep](https://www2.mdpi.com/2624-5175/5/4/51)
- Phasic/tonic tooling: Nagy et al., Sensors 2026;26(4):1389 — EOG-based MAD segmentation + template matching; clinical PSG dataset "lacks manual phasic/tonic annotations" — [MDPI review report](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/4/1389/review_report); SNOOZ toolbox classifies REM mini-epochs into phasic/tonic from detected REMs in EOG — [SNOOZ docs](https://snooz-toolbox-documentation.readthedocs.io/stable/user_guide/tools/Processing/Classify_REMs_Phasic_Tonic_from_EOG.html); automatic REM-event detector (Mednick lab, J Neurosci Methods 2016) "reliably matches human scorers" — [UKY record](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/automatic-detection-of-rapid-eye-movements-rems-a-machine-learnin/)
- Arousal detection algorithms: single-lead EEG stacked ensemble on 994 PhysioNet PSGs improved specificity/sensitivity/AUC by ~8–11% over the best sub-model (2021) — [PMC8467870](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8467870/); Inception-ResNet-v2 + SVM on single-lead EEG, error <8% (2023) — [PMC10275555](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10275555/); RR-interval deep model on 5,323 SHHS recordings detects arousals from ECG alone (CinC 2022) — [CinC 2022 PDF](https://cinc.org/archives/2022/pdf/CinC2022-080.pdf); EEG-free arousal detection from wearable-measurable signals in MESA — [PMC11431496](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11431496/); leg-worn accel+PPG+temperature in 14 children: movement features most informative, HRV less, ROC-AUC 0.94 but arousal-class F1 0.65 — [PMC12136197](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12136197/)
- Sound and REM: Liu & Sheth (NeuroReport 2009) — sufficiently loud sounds evoke a P300 in REM; "in their experience" ≥68 dB causes arousals/awakenings (anecdotal) — [UH PDF](https://www.ece.uh.edu/sites/www.ece/files/faculty/bsheth/Categorization-of-Sound-in-SII-Sleep--Liu-Sheth-NeuroReport-2009.pdf); Stuart & Conduit (Sleep 2009) — sub-waking-threshold tones in REM increased EEG arousal, decreased eye-movement amplitude/frequency and visual-imagery reports — [PMC2647794](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2647794); ascending 40–90 dB protocols used to find awakening thresholds — [Pilon et al.](https://legacy.scnp.org/journal/index.php/SCNP/article/view/81.html)
- Quality guard from data: 16% of amplitude-adequate home ZMax nights showed muscle-band contamination → authors recommend a secondary spectral (muscle-band) filter — [Parry & Briganti 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.01.26354591v1.full)

### Inferences
- An evidence-aligned REMind arousal guard: (i) frontal alpha and 15–30 Hz beta power rise, (ii) high-frequency (EMG-band) power rise on any channel, (iii) IMU movement, (iv) PPG heart-rate jump — any one → pause; resume only after a clean interval and at a lower volume step, as the Nijmegen protocol does.
- Phasic-vs-tonic is practically relevant: cueing during REM-burst (phasic) periods is less likely to arouse (higher thresholds) but phasic periods are also when frontal channels are most EOG-contaminated; a REM-burst detector on AF7/AF8 can therefore serve both as REM evidence and as a "phasic window" indicator, albeit unvalidated against EOG-based phasic/tonic scoring.
- Even sub-threshold tones alter REM physiology (Stuart & Conduit), so a strict volume cap (≤45 dBA background, start at individual threshold, ≤5 dB steps) and logging of every cue are justified.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying a dose–response curve of awakening probability vs dB during REM, and none on arousal guards evaluated on consumer forehead EEG specifically.
- No validation of phasic/tonic classification from frontal EEG alone (only EOG-based tools).
- SleepLoop's own arousal-handling details (Huwiler et al.) were not retrieved.

Scientific Data 2025;12:301, DOI 10.1038/s41597-025-04579-8: two home-sleep studies, 320 nights total, all with ear-EEG, some with scalp EEG and/or wrist actigraphy, all subjects recorded multiple times; license listed as CC BY-NC-ND in the Europe PMC record; "most extensive open access data set available for mobile EEG development" (authors' claim) — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1038/s41597-025-04579-8&format=json&resultType=core); [Aarhus record](https://pure.au.dk/portal/da/publications/ear-eeg-sleep-monitoring-data-sets/); earlier EESM19 (20 subjects × 4 home nights, partial PSG) on OpenNeuro and 80-night feature data on Mendeley — [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/dj6ph9k53p)
- **Muse sleep datasets**: none found on Zenodo, OSF or PhysioNet. Existing public Muse datasets are non-sleep: "Muse EEG Subconscious Decisions Dataset" (20 subjects, CC BY 4.0) — [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.8429739](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.8429739); Muse 2 N400 study on OSF (10.17605/OSF.IO/U6Y9G); NeuroSense Muse 2 emotion dataset with a no-re-identification DUA — [Zenodo 14003181](https://zenodo.org/records/14003181)
- **NSRR** (large PSG cohorts, e.g., SHHS, MESA, CFS used by SleepPPG-Net): signals as EDF, annotations as XML; access requires a per-dataset request/DAUA and token; some datasets (e.g., Wisconsin Sleep Cohort since Oct 2021) restricted to academic-affiliated users — [nsrr R package](https://github.com/oslerinhealth/nsrr); [WSC changelog](https://sleepdata.org/datasets/wsc/pages/CHANGELOG.md); [AASM announcement](https://aasm.org/national-sleep-research-resource-portal-opens-to-promote-collaborative-research/)
- **Benchmarks built on open PSG**: SleepYland (2025) — ~220,000 h in-domain + ~84,000 h out-of-domain, pretrained U-Sleep/DeepResNet/SleepTransformer and the SOMNUS ensemble — [arXiv 2506.08574](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08574v1); Stanford Sleep Bench (Dec 2025) — 17,467 PSGs, >163,000 h, staging/apnea/age tasks, weights and code promised — [arXiv 2512.09591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09591v1); SleepBench/OSF (Mar 2026) — 166,500 h from nine public sources; existing foundation models "fail to generalize to missing channels at inference" — [arXiv 2603.00190](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00190v1)
- **Formats/standards**: EEG-BIDS (Pernet, Appelhoff, Gorgolewski et al., Sci Data 2019) accepts EDF(+) and supports HED tags in events.tsv; OpenNeuro requires BIDS-validator compliance and leaves privacy review to the uploader — [EEG-BIDS PDF](https://brainscan.uwo.ca/research/cores/computational_core/uploads/Sept23%20-%20s41597-019-0104-8.pdf); [OpenNeuro guide](https://conductscience.com/sciencedex/datasets/openneuro); MNE-BIDS implements BEP006 (EEG) — [mne-bids PR #78](https://github.com/mne-tools/mne-bids/pull/78)
- No sleep/PSG BIDS extension proposal appears in the BEP list (closest are BEP044 Stimuli, BEP045 Physio) — [BIDS BEPs](https://bids.neuroimaging.io/extensions/beps.html); [BIDS extensions overview](https://bids-extensions.readthedocs.io/)
- Community practice for hypnograms in BIDS (Neurostars): options are stages in `events.tsv`, HED tags, a separate physio file, `.bidsignore`, or — the solution Kaare Mikkelsen adopted and that passes the validator — a second events file with `acq-scoring`; the thread links BEP021 without describing it — [Neurostars thread 1](https://neurostars.org/t/how-to-add-sleep-scorings/21965); [Neurostars thread 2](https://neurostars.org/t/organize-sleep-stage-scoring-data-in-bids/26085)
- **HED-SCORE** library schema (Hermes, Pal Attia et al., Scientific Data 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41597-025-05791-2): machine-readable SCORE vocabulary for EEG features including a "Sleep and drowsiness" branch (sleep stages and sleep phenomena), compatible with BIDS events.tsv; current version 2.1.0 — [Sci Data PDF](https://www.opendata.uni-halle.de/bitstream/1981185920/122779/1/s41597-025-05791-2.pdf); [HED schemas reference](https://www.hedtags.org/hed-schemas/schemas_reference.html); [SCORE library docs](https://hed-schema-library.readthedocs.io/en/latest/SCORE_library.html)
- **Luna** (Purcell lab) reads NSRR XML and EDF+ and its HYPNO command assumes 30-s epochs starting at EDF start; misaligned stage annotations cause conflicts, especially with gapped EDF+D; Moonlight is its R/Shiny viewer with hypnogram statistics and automated staging — [Luna hypno walkthrough](https://zzz.bwh.harvard.edu/luna-walkthrough/p3/hypno); [Luna hypnograms ref](https://zzz.bwh.harvard.edu/luna/ref/hypnograms); [Moonlight](https://zzz.bwh.harvard.edu/luna/apps/moonlight)
- **YASA** (Vallat & Walker, eLife 2021;10:e70092): LightGBM on a central EEG channel + optional EOG/EMG, 100 Hz, 0.4–30 Hz, trained/validated on >30,000 h of PSG; free and open source — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/70092.pdf); [YASA GitHub](https://github.com/paulroujansky/yasa)

### Inferences
- BOAS is the single most relevant external validation set for REMind: AF7/AF8 frontal EEG at 256 Hz + IMU + PPG with consensus-scored PSG mirrors Muse's frontal channels (TP9/TP10 have no analogue there). Wearanize+ adds home-condition realism and PPG/EDA but requires a DUA.
- A credible REMind validation plan can be fully executed on open data: train/fine-tune on BOAS, test cross-device on Wearanize+ ZMax (as ezscore-f did), and report REM κ/F1 against both consensus and U-Sleep labels.
- For REMind's own exports, EDF(+) + BIDS layout with stages in an `acq-scoring` events.tsv (plus HED-SCORE tags) is the de-facto interoperable choice until a sleep BEP exists; Luna's 30-s-epoch-from-EDF-start assumption argues for epoch-aligned recordings.

## Key Question 8 — Open-science practices and tooling that make a sleep-neurotech repository credible and reusable

### Takeaway
The small projects that gained scientific traction (Portiloop, Dreamento, ezscore-f, YASA, Dreem's open datasets) share a pattern: an open-hardware/open-code release with a permissive license, a quantitative validation against PSG or expert labels published as a preprint or in a software journal (PLoS ONE, SoftwareX, eLife, JOSS), open datasets or benchmark reuse, and — increasingly — preregistration; conversely, uninstallable packaging (ezscore-f in Parry 2026) and undisclosed training data (Hypnodyne ZLab) are explicitly penalised in independent evaluations.

### Cited Findings
- Portiloop (Valenchon et al., PLoS ONE 2022): open-hardware, low-cost closed-loop EEG stimulator built from commercial parts, lightweight neural network with hyperparameter exploration, real-time spindle detection 71% precision/recall, stimulation within ~300 ms, "fully available to the research community as an open science project" on GitHub — [PMC9394839](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9394839/); [arXiv 2107.13473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13473v3)
- Dreamento: MIT-licensed Python toolbox for ZMax (real-time recording, autoscoring, stimulation, TTS, annotations); software paper in SoftwareX 2023;24:101595; validity claims point to the ZMax preprint; used in a multi-centre study whose 2026 version is labelled "preregistered" — [Dreamento GitHub](https://github.com/dreamento/dreamento); [bioRxiv 2024.06.21.600133](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v1.full)
- The Dresler lab framed this stack as "Citizen neuroscience: wearable technology and open software to study the human brain in its natural habitat" (Eur J Neurosci 2024) — [Dresler lab methods](https://dreslerlab.org/methods/)
- ezscore-f released code, models and tutorials (github.com/coonwg1/ezscore) — but an independent 2026 evaluation could not evaluate it "because of installation incompatibilities", a concrete reusability failure — [Parry & Briganti 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.01.26354591v1.full)
- Hypnodyne's ZLab training data are undisclosed, so the open DreamentoScorer and ZLab "were not directly comparable" — transparency of training data is treated as a scientific requirement — [Esfahani et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.08.18.553744.full)
- Dreem published its multi-scored datasets plus MIT code to reproduce paper results (`download_data.py`, example configs) — [dreem-learning-open](https://github.com/Dreem-Organization/dreem-learning-open)
- YASA's eLife paper reports agreement "comparable to human scorers and to interscorer agreement" and reviewers praised accuracy and ease of use; the repository designates the eLife paper as the citation — [eLife reviews](https://elifesciences.org/articles/70092/peer-reviews); [YASA GitHub](https://github.com/paulroujansky/yasa)
- Software-journal venues used by sleep tools: SleepECG (HRV-based staging) in JOSS 2023;8(86):5411, DOI 10.21105/joss.05411 — [JOSS](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.05411); Openseize (EEG big-data processing) JOSS 2023 — [JOSS 5126](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.05126); NeuroDSP JOSS — [JOSS 1272](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.01272); JOSS design described in [arXiv 1707.02264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02264)
- Open evaluation frameworks instead of leaderboards: SleepYland releases pretrained models and ~300k h of evaluation; no live sleep-staging leaderboard was found — [arXiv 2506.08574](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08574v1); SleepFM reportedly reaches staging F1 0.70–0.78, competitive with U-Sleep and YASA (GitHub mirror summary, unverified) — [sleepfm-clinical mirror](https://scour.ing/@emschwartz/p/https://github.com/zou-group/sleepfm-clinical)
- Community/educational neurotech: EEG-Notebooks (now EEG-ExPy) is a NeuroTechX Python library led by John Griffiths (CAMH/U Toronto) building on Barachant's muse-lsl, supporting Muse 2016/2/S, ported ERP-CORE paradigms, run through Google Summer of Code 2021–2022 — no peer-reviewed validation paper found — [Brainhack 2020](https://brainhack.org/global2020/project/project_53/); [Neurostars GSoC 2022](https://neurostars.org/t/gsoc-2022-project-idea-7-2-mobile-eeg-cognitive-neuroscience-experiments-with-the-eeg-notebooks-python-library-175-350-h/21390); [muselsl PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/muselsl)
- PiEEG gained visibility through a series of arXiv papers (2022 robot demo; 2023 EEG/ECG/EMG/EOG shield; 2024 PiEEG-16) plus a 2021 IEEE conference paper and press coverage — [arXiv 2202.01936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01936); [arXiv 2409.07491](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.07491); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/raspberry-pi-on-the-brain)
- pyRiemann's citation of record is a Zenodo software DOI (v0.8, 2025); no JOSS paper confirmed — [pyRiemann docs](https://pyriemann.readthedocs.io/en/v0.12/introduction.html)
- Preregistration: an OSF registration is a frozen, time-stamped plan; preregistration on OSF correlates with more reported null findings (61%), attributed to less analytic flexibility; Registered Reports add peer review before data collection — [OSF help](https://help.osf.io//help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations); [PMC9119902](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9119902); [eTrials testbed](https://www.etrialstestbed.org/resources/preregistration)
- Reproducibility checklist for cognitive EEG (Surrey): standardise to BIDS, share pipelines/scripts, register datasets on OSF — [Surrey checklist PDF](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-06/the-reproducibility-eeg-checklist-creating-checklist-cognitive-eeg-studies-promote-open-science.pdf); EEG-BIDS enables "fully automated data analysis workflows" and FAIR metadata — [EEG-BIDS 2019](https://brainscan.uwo.ca/research/cores/computational_core/uploads/Sept23%20-%20s41597-019-0104-8.pdf)
- Privacy for sharing personal sleep EEG: a 2025 transformer autoencoder produces anonymised EEG that retains utility for ML sleep staging and notes transformer models have already been used for re-identification — [arXiv 2509.20454](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20454v1); a 2024 systematic review covers DP-SGD GANs for synthetic EEG — [arXiv 2412.11394](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.11394); a 2026 framework finds generative/perturbation methods mostly at "basic exposure control/engineering isolation" levels with no unified formal privacy bound — [arXiv 2605.11386](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11386); NeuroSense (Muse dataset) uses a DUA forbidding re-identification — [Zenodo 14003181](https://zenodo.org/records/14003181)
- Device-neutral validation guidance from the field: evaluate with subject-level splits (intra-subject evaluation "systematically inflates" agreement) and test zero-shot transfer to other wearables — [2026 systematic review preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202607.1006)

### Inferences
- Concrete credibility steps for REMind that match what worked elsewhere: (1) pip-installable package with pinned deps and CI (ezscore-f's failure shows installability is judged); (2) a SoftwareX/JOSS paper for the recorder/replay engine and a preprint for the REM detector validated on BOAS/Wearanize+ with subject-level splits and REM κ/F1/latency; (3) MIT/BSD license and a Zenodo DOI per release; (4) preregister the Pilot 4/5 cueing protocols on OSF (the Dresler lab now labels its lucid-dream study "preregistered"); (5) BIDS/EDF+ export with `acq-scoring` events and HED-SCORE tags so YASA/Luna/MNE users can open data; (6) disclose training data and algorithm versions (the ZLab critique).
- Because no public Muse sleep dataset exists, a consented, de-identified, DUA-gated release of Muse S Athena overnight recordings with PSG or expert labels would itself be a notable contribution — synthetic/anonymised EEG is not yet a credible substitute for sharing.

- No publication counts for OpenBCI, and no JOSS paper confirmed for pyRiemann or Portiloop.
- The BIDS BEP021 relevance to sleep annotations remains unverified.
